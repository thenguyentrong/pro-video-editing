# Cutting craft (applies to every style)

## Why a cut works
A cut is invisible when the viewer *wants* the next shot. Give every cut a reason: an action continues, a sound lands, a question needs an answer, a new idea starts. Walter Murch's priorities for a cut, in order: emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, 2D plane of the screen, 3D space. When in doubt, protect emotion and story over continuity.

## The basic moves
| Move | What | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Hard cut | One shot ends, the next starts | 90% of all cuts |
| Cut on action | Cut mid-movement, the next shot continues it | Hides the cut; physical actions |
| Match cut | Shapes, colours or positions match across the cut | Linking two ideas or places |
| J-cut / L-cut | Sound leads (J) or trails (L) the picture | Smooth dialogue and voice-over flow |
| Smash cut | Quiet → loud (or reverse) | Surprise, the drop, a joke |
| Cutaway / insert | Short detail shot, then back | Show what the voice talks about; hide a bad bit |
| Jump cut | Time removed inside one shot | Talking heads, energy (not for calm films) |
| Speed ramp | Fast → slow inside a shot | Hero moment of a machine or athlete |
| Freeze frame | Picture stops | "Wait, what?" beat |
| Dissolve / dip to black | Fade between shots / through black | Time passing; chapter end before an end card |
| Whip / mask / morph | Motion or an object carries you to the next shot | Energy or continuity; use 1–2 times, not as a habit |

## Continuity
180° rule (keep the camera on one side of the action), 30° rule (change angle ≥ 30° or size between two shots of the same subject), eyeline match, screen direction (keep movement left→right if it started that way), and eye trace (the next shot's subject sits where the eye already is, especially in fast montages).

## Shot sizes and order
Wide (where) → medium (who) → close (what matters). Two shots of the same size in a row look like a mistake. Mix sizes; a detail shot after a wide one makes the viewer lean in.

## Rhythm
- Cut on the music: big changes on the downbeat of a bar; normal cuts every 2, 4 or 8 beats; every beat only in a fast montage. 1 beat = 60/BPM s.
- Measured shot lengths per style live in `measured-numbers.md` and each style file. Typical: trailer 1.0–1.9 s, Apple spot 1.1–2.3 s, brand anthem 0.8–1.3 s (luxury up to 2.6 s), lifestyle montage ~1.0 s, design-tech launch 2–4 s, editorial explainer 2.2–5.2 s, robot proof shots 4.3–7.1 s.
- A film needs breathing room: hold 0.5–1 s after the key line; slow down before the end card.

## Story order that survives editing
Hook (1–3 s) → tension/question → evidence → turn (the key point, on the biggest musical moment) → payoff (show it working) → call to action (facts only here). Re-hook every ~7–10 s in anything longer than 30 s.

## Editing process
Selects (best moments only) → stringout → paper edit (order on paper) → assembly (too long) → rough cut (story works; show one person) → fine cut (trim to the frame) → picture lock → sound design + mix → colour → graphics/captions → master export → cutdowns (60/30/15/6 s, 16:9/9:16/4:5) → QC on a phone.
