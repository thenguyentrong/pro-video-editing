# Editorial explainer
**Use it for:** a 2.5-15 min film (documentary explainers run 20-50 min) that explains news, data, a system, a place or an argument, led by a voice (narrator, reporter or host) and proven with real sources on screen. Also the 60-90 s vertical version of the same idea. **Not for:** a product launch, a brand anthem, a 6-15 s social meme (use `social-vertical.md`), or a film with nothing to prove. For the close-up craft of documents, charts and maps, read `documentary-evidence.md` next to this file.

## Signature (one paragraph)
A calm voice carries the film, and every claim gets a real thing on screen: the study, the report page, the satellite image, the other outlet's clip, the object in a hand. Footage is cut at about 2-3 s per shot, but the motion budget goes into one or two long graphics (a map, a chart, a mechanism) that run 8-28 s in one continuous move. There is one accent colour for all annotation (Vox yellow, WSJ teal, NYT white, Bloomberg yellow/crimson, Economist red, Guardian yellow, CNBC gold), every borrowed picture carries a small source line, and cuts sit on the end of a sentence, not on the music beat (cut-on-onset rate is at or below chance in 21 of 22 films measured; the one exception, a DW observational film, cuts on the start of a spoken line). Structure comes from sound: a short drop to near-silence before a new chapter or a hard piece of evidence, then a loud re-entry.

## Structure
Only the first 150 s (newer films†: the first 300 s or the full short film) was measured; after that, repeat the evidence loop (beats 4-7) once per chapter.
1. **Cold open, 0-13 s (≈0-3 % of a 6-8 min film).** The proof before the question: an object strip (Vox, 13.1 s), the reporter at the desk with the evidence on his monitors (NYT, 0-8.4 s), raw footage or a wordless open (Johnny Harris, 0-8 s), the reporter on screen in second 1 (Bloomberg Investigates), the subject in her own voice for 9.5 s (Guardian†), one animated chart for 11.4 s (CNBC†), a recording with its source and date (CNBC investigation†). Documentaries open longer: 20-40 s of news clips or montage (DW†, FT Film†). No logo, except a 2-3 s house ident in documentaries (FT, DW "DocFilm").
2. **The question (≈2-5 %).** Host or narrator says what the film will answer. The Economist puts the host's question to camera with a lower third at 0-6 s.
3. **First evidence burst (≈3-8 %).** 4-7 cuts per 10 s of archive, news clips and headlines, with 1-4-word keyword captions.
4. **Title or series sting (≈5-20 %).** Measured at 21-95 s: NYT title card 21-26 s, Vox sting 35-80 s (3.4-4.7 s long), Bloomberg ident ≈68 s and episode title ≈95 s, NYT Opinion title 82 s. New films†: CNBC series title at 31 s (4:26 film), 107 s (10:20) and 159 s (21:13), i.e. 12-17 % in; documentaries 58-107 s (DW 58-94 s, FT Film 107 s). Follow it with 1-2 s of near-silence and a hard re-entry.
5. **Mechanism.** One long graphic (18-20 s, Vox; 8-16 s per map move, WSJ) explains how the thing works. No cuts inside.
6. **Guide on screen.** The reporter or host returns every 20-40 s for 2-7 s (WSJ; CNBC† every 20-50 s for 3-8 s; a remote Guardian† correspondent 6 times in 169 s). Experts appear as honest webcam frames or clean boxless interviews. A data presenter (FT†) stays on screen beside the chart instead of cutting away.
7. **Primary document, then the person behind it.** The study map, the report page or the filing with one line highlighted, then its author or the official.
8. **Chapter turn.** Chapter card (question form works) on darkened footage, with a 0.5-2 s music drop before it.
9. **Close.** Land the answer on the strongest piece of evidence or on the host; keep end branding short.

## Picture
- Real footage stays close to its source look. No unifying grade on third-party clips (WSJ, Bloomberg keep other broadcasters' chyrons and logos). Own footage: naturalistic, slightly desaturated (Bloomberg), warm practicals vs cool window light (Johnny Harris).
- Archive is desaturated and lifted so captions read: `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(.8) brightness(1.15)` (Vox). It comes in bursts of 4-6 cuts.
- Reporter sets: a real desk or newsroom, 2-3 angles (wide, close, over the shoulder on screens), cursor-visible screen recordings, a finger on the monitor, paper in hand (NYT). WSJ adds a profile on black.
- Graphics live in one world per film: grey creased paper or off-white graph paper (Vox), pale basemap (NYT), desaturated satellite texture with dark seas (WSJ), dark slate 3D maps (Bloomberg Investigates), antique paper with hatching (Johnny Harris), black chart with one red (Economist), real objects on one black desk lit like a product shot (Guardian tabletop†), dark-teal radial ground for every card, chart and map (CNBC†), slate `#262A33` charts (FT†), painted desaturated illustrations for recreations (CNBC investigation†).
- One accent colour for all annotation: Vox `#FEFF0F`; NYT white (yellow `#FFE600` only as a document highlighter); WSJ teal `#1FB5B5`/`#2EC4C9` with crimson `#C8102E` only for an adversary and orange `#E8601C` for one threatened point; Bloomberg yellow `#E8C33F` + crimson `#D0263A`; Economist red `#E3120B`; Johnny Harris yellow `#FFFF4C`; Guardian yellow ≈`#FFE500`†; CNBC gold ≈`#F2B400` with cyan ≈`#3EC1E6` only for data†; FT Film acid yellow ≈`#E4F53C` only for title and place cards†. FT data charts are the exception: several series colours (yellow, cyan, blue, crimson, grey for context) with direct labels.
- Borrowed sources can be retyped into the house card system instead of shown as screenshots (CNBC†: headlines, posts, press releases, all on the same ground with one gold highlight; FT†: quotes as typed cards with a round portrait and credit).
- Sensitive testimony: painted illustrations (CNBC†) or macro and silhouettes (DW†), never actors with blurred faces.
- Category colours are set once and kept for the whole film (Vox: red vs green for the two sides).
- No grain, light leaks or letterbox in the newsroom styles. Johnny Harris is the exception: scanlines, paper grain, halation, 2.39:1 recreations.

## Transitions
- Hard cuts almost everywhere. No whips, no glitch transitions (except the NYT Opinion glitch state for missing data).
- Inside a graphic, the cut is a scale punch-in: three hard 0.5 s scale sets on a map before the full view (Vox).
- Device montages, used once: Bloomberg's stutter montage (8-12 clips, 3 frames each); Johnny Harris's word match-cut (12-14 aligned scans, 4-6 frames each).
- Chapter titles fade in and out in about 0.5 s (Bloomberg). Dissolves and light leaks only in Johnny Harris map films and around documentary titles (DW†).
- Presenter-to-chart changes can be short pushes or slides (FT†); a scene detector misses them, so measure such films with an adaptive detector.
- One effect transition per film at most, kept for the series title (CNBC† glitch into "AI Insights"); chapter labels can be typed on with a cursor bar (CNBC†).

## Sound
- Voice on top, dry and clear. Cut on sentence ends; let a clip's own sound enter hard for about 1 s, then duck (Vox: rises of 30-44 dB in 50 ms).
- Pick one bed policy: continuous score, never below -50 dBFS (Bloomberg, WSJ), or silence between phrases in the hardest parts (NYT investigations, Economist debate). A data presenter can run completely dry: FT† explainers have 32-52 gaps per 5 min down to ≈-80 dBFS and no music under the charts.
- Two loudness registers†: newsroom explainers -21 to -27 LUFS (CNBC -23.4 to -24.0, Guardian -21.3 to -27.1, FT data -23.6 to -27.3); documentaries -14.8 to -16.3 LUFS (FT Film, DW).
- Structure through dips, not hits: 0.5-2 s drop before a section, then +15 to +32 dB on the new section.
- Delivery: the films sit at -14 to -24 LUFS. For web/social delivery master at about -14 to -16 LUFS (WSJ and Economist both point to -16 for social); keep the bed 10-15 dB under the voice.

## Do / Don't
- Do prove each claim with its primary source on screen, then show the person behind it.
- Do give the long shots to maps and mechanisms (8-28 s, one continuous move) and keep footage at about 2-3 s.
- Do use one accent colour and a small source line on every borrowed image.
- Do mark chapters with a short silence and a typed or serif question card.
- Don't cut to the beat; don't add music hits under every sentence.
- Don't caption every word in 16:9; keyword captions are 1-4 words.
- Don't restyle other outlets' footage; keep their branding and add a credit.
- Don't use stock as proof. Generic stock may illustrate a word, never a claim.
- Do put a statistic on top of the place it describes, one number per card, ≈5 s (Guardian†).
- Do keep one card system for every outside source (headline, post, press release, quote) so many outlets read as one film (CNBC†, FT†).
- Do let a data chart be the long shot (9-26 s), built in steps with direct labels and a source line (FT†).
- Do bring the series title after the stakes are set: 12-17 % into a long film (CNBC†, FT Film†).
- Don't put music under a data presenter talking through a chart (FT†).
- Don't recreate testimony with actors; use real recordings over illustrations or silhouettes (CNBC†, DW†).
- Don't speed up a business explainer to look modern: CNBC† keeps ASL 5-6 s and ≤ 3 cuts per 10 s in every quarter.

## Recipe
A 3-4 min explainer in Hyperframes (1920x1080, 25 fps). Real footage + licensed stock + HTML/GSAP graphics.
1. **Script by evidence.** For each claim, write the proof asset next to it (study, report page, satellite still, clip with credit). No proof, no claim.
2. **Record the voice first** (or a timing guide). Mark sentence ends; these are the cut points.
3. **Source footage.** Stock queries (Pexels, Pixabay; archive from Wikimedia Commons, public-domain only, with credit): `city aerial dusk drone`, `container port aerial`, `factory assembly line wide`, `hands typing keyboard close up`, `trading screens close up`, `printed document on desk close up`, `newspaper printing press`, `satellite view of earth` (NASA library, credit NASA). Search for what is in the picture, not for the idea.
4. **Cold open (0-10 s):** one object or document shot of 8-13 s with a slow move (`scale 1 → 1.05`, `ease:"none"`), or 4-6 archive cuts of 0.75-1.5 s.
5. **Ground layer:** footage full-bleed; archive gets `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(.8) brightness(1.15)`.
6. **Keyword caption** (Vox look): `font: 900 8.8vh/1.05 "Archivo Black"; color:#FEFF0F; text-transform:uppercase; text-align:center`; hard on with the word, hold 1.5-3 s.
7. **Lower third** (pick one house and keep it): NYT white box `padding:1.2vh 1.6vh`, name `700 4vh "Source Serif 4"`, role `500 1.6vh "Libre Franklin"; letter-spacing:.08em; uppercase`; fade 0.3 s, hold 4-5 s, placed in the subject's open space.
8. **Credit line on every borrowed shot:** `font: 400 1.5vh "Libre Franklin"; left:3%; bottom:4%; color:#fff`. WSJ variant: three lines top-left (source caps 1.9 % H, place 3 % H, date 3 % H).
9. **One long mechanism graphic (18-20 s):** SVG map or chart, one continuous camera move `scale 1 → 2.2` over 10-12 s `power1.inOut`; lines drawn with `strokeDashoffset` over 1-4 s; labels fade 0.3-0.4 s, stagger 0.3 s.
10. **Title or sting at 20-60 s:** 3-5 s, then 1-2 s near-silence (`data-automation` volume to about -45 dBFS) and a hard re-entry on the next shot.
11. **Chapter card:** footage darkened ≈50 %; kicker `700 3.8vh Inter uppercase`; question `600 8vh "Playfair Display"`; ≈4 s; music drop 0.5-2 s before it.
12. **Mix:** voice ≈-16 to -20 LUFS short-term, bed 13-15 dB under; clips enter at full level for ≈1 s; master `loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=11` for web.
13. **Check:** ASL of footage parts 2-3 s, one graphic of 8-28 s per 2-3 min, every borrowed frame credited, no cut lands mid-sentence.

## Variants
- **Vox explainer.** Voice-led, a paper world for all graphics (grey creased `#E7E7E7` or graph paper `#FAFBF6`), real scans and objects as the spine, yellow `#FEFF0F` as the only highlight, other outlets' clips as witnesses, logo sting at 35-80 s with a 1-2 s silence after it. ASL 2.2-5.0 s; median 1.5-4.2 s.
  - Four sub-forms: host-led history (producer on camera, archive bursts of 0.75-1.5 s), narrator + primary documents (no host, drone b-roll, study pages), science from the news (slowest, median 4.2 s), civics data on paper (busiest, median 1.5 s).
  - Maps use a 5-step single-hue ramp; credit every borrowed map in a corner at ≈2.4 % H.
- **NYT visual investigation.** Reporter at a real desk, method on screen (screen recordings with the cursor, finger on satellite images), white 2 px boxes and serif labels, comparison cards every ≈2.2 s, documents on black with one yellow line, credit on every clip, silence around the hardest images. ASL ≈5 s, median ≈3 s, no shot under 1 s, proof held 14-28 s. Loudness -17 to -20 LUFS.
- **Bloomberg / WSJ business.** Bloomberg: 2 s median b-roll on a continuous score, own newsroom as evidence (TV clip with live ticker, headline cards over footage darkened ≈50 %), charts composited on real surfaces, line drawn over 20-25 s. WSJ: reporter as guide every 20-40 s, promise first (the company's own renders) then proof (leaked document, satellite before/after), teal annotations, scale comparisons on familiar places, one continuous move per map.
