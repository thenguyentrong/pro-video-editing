# Movie trailer
**Use it for:** film trailers, teasers, TV spots, season trailers for a streamed series, and any promo that should feel like "a film is coming" (a product or event sold as a story with stakes). **Not for:** explainers that must teach step by step, product demos where every feature needs a label, calm brand films (use the Apple or anthem style), or pieces under ~15 s (use the TV-spot variant or a social style).

## Signature
A trailer is a short promise told in three acts: a cold open that shows the world, a studio or brand logo as a beat, an escalating middle, one hard stop, then the title and a short end block. The pace in the body is fast and almost flat (about one shot per second), but the trailer breathes with held wide shots, black holds and one measured silence before the biggest hit. Text is rare: a handful of cards in the whole piece, 1 to 4 words each, in one typeface that belongs to the film, not to a template. Every 16:9 piece sits inside a cinema letterbox. The premium feeling comes from contrast (1 s shots against 8 to 24 s payoffs, silence against a +30 dB hit) and from restraint, not from effects.

## Structure
Percent of total runtime, from the measured trailers. In short: build up, build up, build up, then stop.
1. **Cold open, 0–11/22 %.** 16–33 s of story before any logo (Marvel 11–17 %, A24 19–22 %, Warner 14–22 %). Either slow (ASL ≈3.5 s, long shots split by 1.6–1.9 s of black) or fast (ASL ≈1.0 s, ending on a +25 dB hit on the logo).
2. **Logo as a beat, at 9–22 %.** 1.2–2.9 s. On a hit, in silence, or rebuilt from the film's world (letters turning to dust, a logo made of the film's material). Final trailers and spots put a customised logo first (0–3 s). † Also seen: logos as late as 32 % (Bone Temple), a distributor logo built as a prop in the set (Hokum, 21 %), logos first and plain for an auteur epic (Odyssey, 6–13 %), or no logo at all (Disclosure Day teaser and spot).
3. **Act 1, the world, to ≈33 %.** ASL ≈1.1–1.4 s. Director or "from the makers of" credit at 32–37 %.
4. **Date or month card, at 33 % (Marvel) or 56–62 % (Warner).** The month only, not the full date.
5. **Act 2, escalation.** Taglines at 64–75 %, often split into one-word or two-part cards with footage between them. Densest cutting: 30–75 % (Marvel), 60–80 % (streaming), 70–91 % (A24, Warner).
6. **One hard stop, 1.5–6 s.** Black plus silence, a held lonely wide, or a quiet dialogue beat. A quiet reveal can run 6.5–13 s at −39 to −45 LUFS.
7. **Title, at 83–96 % (most at 86–94 %).** On the biggest hit or on the decay of the last impact. Hold 2.1–6.6 s. † Earlier (73–76 %) only when a scene, scare or gag follows the title (Exit 8, Gatto); † up to 8.3 s when a character animates through the logo.
8. **End block, 5–20 s.** Date card 1.3–4 s, a comic "button" scene of 4–16 s, cast billing, or a ticking countdown.

## Picture
- **Subjects:** faces in close-up as a roll call (0.8–1.5 s each), one hero object or place per act, scale wides with a tiny figure (subject under 10 % of frame height) held 2.5–3.7 s every 20–30 s while the rest cuts at ≈1.2 s.
- **Camera:** locked-off wides and slow push-ins in the open; handheld in action; one long payoff take of 8–24 s (slow-motion hero shot, a pull-back, a quiet face).
- **Shot sizes:** wide (where) → close (who) → macro insert (what matters). Never two shots of the same size in a row.
- **Grade:** low-key. Most frames are dark with one hot practical source (window, fire, flare). Colour coded per world or act (teal-steel, amber fire, blue-white), ending on the film's colour. A24 keeps the film's own grade and adds nothing.
- **Texture:** light film grain; letterbox bars on every shot and every card.
- **Aspect:** 2.39:1 (bars ≈12.9 % of height each), 2.20:1, 2.00:1 (≈5.5 %), 1.90:1 or 1.85:1, constant for the whole trailer.

## Type
- **One face per trailer, from the film's world.** All font names are look-alike guesses from frames.
  - Extended heavy caps → free: Michroma. Wide geometric caps → Josefin Sans or Jost. Condensed chrome caps → Barlow Condensed. Humanist title case for a teaser → Source Sans 3.
  - Tiny epic caps (Dune): Michroma, cap 2.5–2.9 % H, tracking ≈0.35 em, centred at 49–50 % H.
  - Huge blunt caps (comedy): Big Shoulders Display Black or Anton, cap 16–17 % H, tracking 0.
  - A24 systems: tracked semibold caps (Montserrat 600, 0.3 em); printed serif with an italic lowercase lead-in (Cormorant Garamond); ultra-condensed heavy caps at 41–68 % H (Anton).
  - Streaming: the show's own font for body cards; a light eyebrow (≈3.5 % H) over a bold date (6.5–8.5 % H) for the platform card (Inter 300 + Inter 800).
- **Case and tracking:** caps, wide tracking 0.25–0.35 em for quiet styles; 0 for huge styles. Never medium size: either tiny and wide or huge and blunt.
- **Placement:** centred, inside the letterbox. Taglines can sit on black, on a brand gradient or glow, or inside a shot.
- **In / out:** mostly a hard cut in and a hard cut out. Allowed slow moves: title fade from black 0.8–1.2 s `power1.out`; letters flicker into place over 0.6–1.5 s; 0.2 s light sweep; the next shot dissolves in under the text over 0.6–0.9 s; credit stacks build one line every 0.5–1.0 s; a date typed one token per ≈1 s; a word that starts at scale 1.4 and settles in 0.4 s `expo.out`. Never `back`, `elastic` or `bounce`.

## Transitions
- Hard cuts for about 95 % of all cuts.
- Black holds 0.8–2.7 s, 1–3 per trailer (epics: 5–10 short dips of 0.4–1.7 s as chapter breaks), with 0.5–0.8 s fades around them.
- At most one cross-dissolve per trailer (out of a card or into the last act).
- Flash frames of 0.2 s only inside the fastest montage.
- Title: a hard cut on the hit, or a fade-up from black.

## Sound
- **Music:** 2–3 cues (open, faster, big) that step up after each new fact. The score rises about +5 LU from the first to the last quarter while the cutting stays flat (Warner). Choose a theme with some risk; an off-period pop needle-drop is an A24 move.
- **Voice:** dialogue lines and in-story voice-over on top, not an announcer (A24 has a voice from the first second, at −20…−23 LUFS short-term). The minimal critics' teaser has no speech at all.
- **Beat sync:** only event trailers, spots and promos cut to the music (27–36 % of cuts on onsets vs 18–19 % chance; † live-action 30–60 s spots 37–41 % vs 26–31 %). Story trailers cut to lines and actions († 24–29 % vs 19–33 %, i.e. near chance). † Slapstick in animation cuts on every hit (58 % vs 29 %).
- **SFX:** drone bed under everything; risers that peak on the exact frame of impact; hits built from three layers (transient, body, sub); a whoosh on fast camera moves; duck the bed under each hit. A braam at most once.
- **Drop-outs:** the silence makes the hit. Pre-title: 0.35 s near −55 dBFS, hit +28…+31 dB, title cut 0.6–2.7 s later. Comic dead air 0.5–0.85 s before each joke.
- **Ducking:** duck music in ≈4 frames, bring it back over 20–30 frames. A sync 2 frames late can feel better than perfect.
- **Mix:** −14 LUFS integrated (−17 for a quiet art-house cut), true peak −1.5 dBTP; loudest momentary −7.4 to −13.5 LUFS at the climax; fade out under the end card.

## Do / Don't
**Do**
- Give the cold open 15–25 % of the runtime before any logo.
- Keep 2–9 text moments in a 2.5 min trailer and say each point once.
- Hold your widest images 2.5–3.7 s while the body cuts at 1.1–1.4 s.
- Put one measured silence before the biggest hit, and land the title on it or on its decay.
- Build every card, logo and end block from the film's world (one face, one colour, one background).
- Slow down after the climax: title 2.7–4.1 s, then a short end block.
- † Put prestige (laurels, critics' quotes, "from the director of") inside a shot or in the film's own type: over blurred footage, typed like a terminal, as a sign in the set.
- † In a 30–60 s spot, split the tagline over 3 cards and write the date and formats over action shots, two words per shot.
- † In horror, use 2–6 s of near-silence early (from ≈20 %), not only before the title.
- † In animation, open on a joke, cut on the dialogue and give one character beat a 5 s hold.

**Don't**
- Don't put a card on every line or explain the plot in text.
- Don't use medium-sized centred Title Case with a drop shadow; it reads as a template.
- Don't accelerate into the end card, and don't add a "wall of noise" without drops.
- Don't use slides, zooms or bouncy eases on cards.
- Don't add flashes and whooshes on every cut.
- Don't drop the letterbox on cards; the bars stay on everything (exceptions measured: full-frame black cards at Universal and Lucasfilm, no bars at all in Pixar's main trailer †).
- † Don't cut a teaser like a short trailer: teasers hold one scene (7.8–24.5 s takes) and show fewer facts.
- † Don't copy hot masters: 5 of the round-3 uploads peak at +0.2…+1.4 dBTP; deliver true peak −1 dBTP. Quiet uploads (a talent spot at −19.9 LUFS, a regional spot at −21.3) are not loudness references.

## Recipe
Target: a 60–70 s blockbuster-style trailer, 1920×1080, 24 fps, real or licensed stock footage plus HTML/GSAP compositions (Hyperframes).
1. **Frame.** Two black bar divs on top of everything: 138 px each for 2.39:1, 104 px for 2.20:1, 60 px for 2.00:1. Grade every clip with `filter: contrast(1.08) saturate(.9) brightness(.92)` plus a vignette `radial-gradient(ellipse 75% 70% at 50% 50%, transparent 55%, rgba(0,0,0,.55))` and grain at opacity 0.06–0.10.
2. **Lay the text cards first** as anchors, then the music cues, then the picture.
3. **Timeline (66 s):**
   - 0–8 s cold open: 1–2 long shots (4–6 s), slow push `scale 1→1.06`, `ease:"none"`; drone bed and one strike.
   - 8–9.5 s black 1.5 s while the strike rings out.
   - 9.5–11 s brand logo on a hit (optional rack-focus: `blur 14px→0` in 0.4 s).
   - 11–24 s Act 1, ASL ≈1.4 s. 24–25.5 s tagline card 1 on a hit.
   - 25.5–44 s Act 2, ASL 0.8–1.0 s, 10–12 cuts per 10 s. One scale wide of 2.5–3 s in the middle.
   - 44–45.5 s tagline card 2. 45.5–52 s one held payoff shot (5–8 s), music down to −35…−45 LUFS.
   - 52–52.35 s full mute. 52.35–55 s hit +28…+31 dB and 2–3 shots of the biggest action.
   - 55–59 s title 3.5–4 s. 59–64 s button (0.5–0.8 s of dead air before the last line) or a typed date. 64–66 s date card 1.6–2 s, fade to silence.
4. **Tagline card.** Centred; main line `font: 600 72px/1 "Josefin Sans"; letter-spacing:.28em; text-transform:uppercase; color:#ece6d6`; connector word 38 px above. `tl.set(card,{opacity:1},t); tl.set(card,{opacity:0},t+1.4)`.
5. **Epic card (alternative).** `.txt{font-family:Michroma;color:#FFC08F}` small line 20 px, main 29 px, `letter-spacing:.35em`, on `radial-gradient(ellipse 80% 90% at 50% 100%,#E2761E,#A23113 45%,#2C080A 80%,#0D0101)`. Letters `from {opacity:0}` with `stagger:{each:.03,from:"random"}, ease:"steps(3)"` (0.6 s); the next shot fades in under the text over 0.8 s, starting 0.4 s before the card ends.
6. **Title.** Fade from black (`opacity 0→1`, 0.8 s, `power1.out`), or a 3D pull-back (`perspective:1200px`, `scale 3.2→1`, `z -800→0`, 4 s `power2.out`, rim glow `drop-shadow(0 0 18px <brand>88)`).
7. **Typed date and countdown.** One `<span>` per token, `opacity 0→1` in 0.15 s, 1 s apart. Compute countdown digits from timeline time (seek-safe); one tick SFX per second.
8. **Sound.** Volume automation lanes for the 0.35 s mute (8 frames at 0) and the quiet scene (−40 dB). Master `loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=11`.
9. **Stock queries:** "aerial desert tiny figure", "lone figure wide landscape fog", "silhouette backlit window smoke", "close up eyes low key", "hand macro dark", "city night rain slow motion", "fire embers slow motion", "storm clouds timelapse dark", "running corridor handheld", "explosion debris slow motion" (only licensed clips; no AI faces).

## Variants
- **Blockbuster, classic main trailer** (Spider-Man): 16 s action cold open → logos at 11–14 % → month card at 33 % → flat ASL 1.0–1.1 s in every quarter → silence → hit → title → 16 s comedy button → tickets and date.
- **Blockbuster, event trailer** (Doomsday): slow–fast–fast–slow (quarter ASL 3.3 · 1.4 · 1.5 · 5.1 s); three long single shots split by black before the logo at ≈17 %; a roll call of faces at 0.8–1.5 s cut to the score; no taglines; a 16.6 s quiet closing scene; 3D title, typed date, countdown.
- **Blockbuster, epic** (Dune): flat ASL 1.37–1.42 s; tiny wide-tracked cards on a glow or haze plate; next shot dissolves under the text; scale wides held 2.5–3.7 s; densest at 82–91 %; one hard stop; title at 94 %.
- **Blockbuster, character teaser** (60–80 s): 6–8 shots of 3.5–8 s, then one ≈21 s payoff take; no action; one small line on black built word by word (groups 2.5 s apart, 0.75–1.0 s fades), then a date and a countdown.
- **TV spot, 30 s** (Endgame): the full arc compressed ≈5×: an 8 s slow open (a customised logo, 2.9 s, then 2 empty wides), 9 s montage at ASL 0.82 s, 1 s black, 3.3 s silent tableau, +30.7 dB hit on the end card; −13.5 LUFS.
- **Vertical 9:16:** same edit and mix, re-framed per shot with keyframed `object-position`; no bars; title at ≈80 % width at ≈38 % height; all text between 15 % and 82 % of the height.
- **A24 art-house, genre event** (Civil War): 27 s dialogue cold open, logo in silence, one-word tagline cards (0.7–0.9 s) between ≈1 s shots, a 13 s music-free stand-off as the turn, climax ASL 0.91 s, a 7.3 s slow aerial before the title (fade-up 1.2 s).
- **A24, slow burn** (Midsommar): ASL falls every quarter (3.1 → 2.0 → 1.3 → 0.9 s), last 6 s at 0.25–0.40 s per shot, hard cut to black; printed serif cards held 2.3–3.3 s.
- **A24, critics' grid / metronome** (Zone of Interest, 60 s): picture / black quote card / picture every ≈2.0 s, a thump on every cut (69 % of cuts on onsets), no dialogue, locked-off symmetric wides, −21 LUFS.
- **A24, star character with loud type** (Marty Supreme): 32 s comic cold open; single words at 41–68 % H for 0.6–0.8 s; pop needle-drop; fastest stretch ASL 0.69 s at 68–82 %; 12 s button with credits building over the footage.
- **Streaming series, final season** (Squid Game, Stranger Things): quiet cold open 3–14 s at −26…−44 dBFS; the platform sting rebuilt from the show's sets after the cold open (2.2 s, +25…+33 dB); corner bug for the first 4–5 s; 3–4 cards in the show's font; a statement card (2.0–2.4 s); the title as the show's own logo animation (6.3–6.6 s); date cards; end logo.
- **Streaming, theatrical film** (Narnia): no corner bug and no opening platform logo; credits composited inside shots; studio-style end block; the platform logo recoloured to the film's palette at the very end.
- **Streaming, live event promo** (Tudum): logo first; kinetic word stacks that grow ≈1.8× per line, a new word every 0.17–0.5 s; ASL 1.0 s, cut to the beat; 1 s of black at −62 dBFS, then the logo on a +54 dB hit.
- † **Streaming, season teaser** (Ahsoka S2): platform bug for the whole piece; studio logo rebuilt in red light at 21 %; two cards inside the 2.39:1 bars on a nebula plate; a 6 s fog section with 2.5–3.3 s shots as a rest; 0.5 s drop-out and +32 dB before the last act; title at 94 %, platform logo 3.3 s.
