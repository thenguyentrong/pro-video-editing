# Construction / AEC
**Use it for:** films about building things: a new construction tool or machine, a construction robot working on a real site, a customer case study on one project, a project or megaproject documentary, a contractor's brand or recruiting film, a pitch or grant film for a construction-tech startup. **Not for:** a pure robot capability demo off-site (use `industrial-robotics.md`, which also covers long uncut proof takes), architecture shown only as finished beauty (use `design-tech.md` or `opening-credits.md`), a voice-led argument about the industry (use `editorial-explainer.md`).

## Signature
The site is the set and the crew is the cast. Every worker on screen wears full PPE (hard hat, hi-vis, glasses, gloves), and the site stays dirty: puddles, shoring, rebar, dust. Size is never claimed, it is shown: a person next to a printed wall, a figure tiny on a crane boom, a drone reveal of a whole yard or community. Claims come from named people on the site, in their own words: superintendent, BIM manager, operator, customer director. The tool or machine is shot from its own height (low, wide, close to the slab), with gloved hands, dust and sparks in close-up. Data appears as the real thing first (plan on a tablet, cab screen, stencils on a counterweight) and only then, if at all, as one clean card. The brand frame is plain: white or black type, one brand colour that already lives on the machine or the jackets (Hilti red, Dusty orange, Liebherr and Built yellow). Launch films move fast (ASL ≈1.7-2.3 s); case studies and documentaries slow down to let people talk (ASL ≈2.5-5.2 s).

## Structure
**Default: site case study, 2-3 min** (Dusty × DPR, Liebherr × SMC, Bechtel PJET):
1. **Open on the site (0-5 %).** Co-brand card (both logos on white, 1 px divider) or a title box over a wide of the real site with a landmark. A wide that shows the place is checkable.
2. **Who and where (5-20 %).** The customer's director or superintendent in PPE, machine behind, name + role super. Project in one sentence (what is built, where).
3. **The problem (20-35 %).** Told by field people in operational words: rails, water level, trades snapping their own lines, a remote island. B-roll of the hard part.
4. **The tool in the workflow (35-65 %).** Real UI on a tablet or cab screen, then the same thing happening in one real shot (8-11 s). Total station, plan, crew reading the result.
5. **One concrete story (65-85 %).** A checkable moment: an off-position pipe found a month early; the hook stays straight while the barge tilts. Add one number if anyone says one.
6. **Verdict + warm close (85-98 %).** The customer says they would use it again, a smile, a sunset aerial or the machine's hero shot.
7. **Logo (last 2-5 %).** On white, 3-5 s, silence or a single hit.

**Launch order** (Jaibot, FieldPrinter 2, Nuron): stutter hook or vision line (0-10 %) → pain shot: a worker drilling overhead in dust (≈45-60 %) or the product name standing on the slab (≈10 %) → black + silence → reveal on the hit → claim blocks, one claim per shot → the one spec at ≈80-86 % ("1/16 in accuracy") → logo in silence.

**Documentary order** (Wolf Ranch, Multi-story robot): outside proof first (press clips, partner executive, 0-9 %) → chapter title → the deal or the problem told by field and design staff → logistics and build → scale from the air → the people who use it → final aerial 15-17 s → logo white on black.

## Picture
- **PPE on everyone, always.** Hard hat, hi-vis, safety glasses, gloves. A film with one bare head on site looks unsafe and amateur.
- **Scale cue in every big shot:** a person next to the wall, a figure on a boom, a pickup beside the robot, dozens of slabs from 60-120 m up. Renders without people use dimension lines instead (ICON: dotted HUD guides with 27 ft, 100 ft, 40 ft).
- **Machine height:** small robots shot with the lens 5-30 cm above the slab, wide lens, slow tracking (Dusty). Big machines from low angles up the boom, hook against the sun (Liebherr).
- **Hands and dust inserts:** gloved hand slams a battery, drill bit in concrete, sparks, bead of printed concrete, macro of the track on timber blocks. 0.5-2 s each.
- **Proof of autonomy:** empty cab, cab POV with no driver, one operator in a truck with a laptop, a split screen of operator and robot (Built).
- **Real data surfaces:** BIM model on a monitor → plan on the robot's tablet → the robot at work (Hilti). Cab touchscreen with a finger on it (Liebherr). Counterweight stencils "10t / 14t" are data too.
- **Grade:** neutral daylight, natural concrete grey; the brand colour pops because everything else is grey. Optional golden hour or sunrise for brand films (Liebherr teal-orange). No stylised grade on proof footage.
- **Real vs render:** ICON's launch is all render, unlabelled; Built composites a blue "data scan" onto real piles, unlabelled. Copy the look, not the silence: label renders and visualisations.

## Transitions
- Hard cuts almost everywhere. Dissolves of ≈0.5 s only inside interview b-roll (Bechtel 2013, Dusty co-brand card).
- Black as punctuation: 2.46 s black before a reveal (Jaibot); 1.1-1.9 s black holds between chapters (Bechtel 2026); short black dips in a 1 s stutter intro (Dusty).
- Brand wipes once per block, not per shot: red chevron wipe (Hilti), yellow strip wipe on the title box (Liebherr).
- Cut on camera motion through dust or particles in render films (ICON).
- End: cut or fade to the logo; white card for brand films (Liebherr 5-13 s, Bechtel 3-5 s), white-on-black for tech (ICON 6 s, Built 3.7-9.9 s).

## Sound
- **Case study / documentary:** lavalier interviews over a soft bed, cuts on sentences, not beats (Dusty DPR: 26 % of cuts on onsets vs 53 % chance). Site sound under b-roll: diesel, reverse beepers, the robot's motors. Measured -21.1 to -14.3 LUFS (docs -13 LUFS, but clipping above 0 dBTP).
- **Launch:** music-led, cuts on bars (Dusty ≈2 s bars at ≈120 BPM; Jaibot 64 % of cuts on onsets vs 33 % chance). One silence before the reveal, then the hit (Jaibot 1.1 s; Dusty 0.45 s). Logo often in silence.
- **Anthem:** one VO voice or a relay of employees, one line each; 0.65 s drop before the last line; hits on word slams.
- **Loudness target:** master to -14 LUFS, -1 dBTP. Several references clip (Liebherr Bauma -10.0 LUFS at +0.3 dBTP, ICON films +0.5 to +2.4 dBTP): do not copy that.

## Do / Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Put every person on site in full PPE | Show one bare head, flip-flops or a phone at the edge of an excavation |
| Put a person or vehicle next to every big thing | Show a wall or machine with nothing to measure it against |
| Let named site staff (with role and company) say the claims | Let an anonymous narrator claim "faster, cheaper, safer" |
| Show the real plan/UI, then the same task in one real shot (8-11 s) | Show only renders, or composite data glows without a label |
| Give safety its own beat: E-stop pressed on camera, tape, sign, PPE (Built: ≈20 % of runtime) | Hide how the machine stays safe around people |
| One claim per shot; one spec per shot | Stack three numbers on one card |
| Label timelapses and speed changes ("Timelapse 20×") | Speed up printing or driving silently (none of the references labels it) |
| Keep the site real: mud, puddles, cables, other trades working | Clean the site until it looks staged |
| Pick one brand colour that is already on the kit | Add gradients, glow or a second accent |

## Recipe
1. **Write the claim list.** 3-5 claims, each with one shot that proves it on a real site, one named person who can say it, and if possible one number (time, accuracy, cost, safety record).
2. **Shot list for one camera + one drone, one site day** (mirrorless or phone on a gimbal, tripod, 2 lavaliers, a licensed drone pilot with site permission; 25p or 24p everywhere; lock white balance; shoot sunrise and golden hour for the wides):
   - D1 drone reveal: rise from behind a stack or fence to the whole site, 15-20 s (one take per chapter)
   - D2 drone top-down over the work zone with people visible, 10 s, static or slow crab
   - D3 drone orbit of the machine at work at 30-40 m, 20 s
   - D4 drone final pull-back at golden hour, 20 s (the ending)
   - G1 wide of the site with a landmark or the building, tripod, 8 s
   - G2 the machine doing one full task cycle, locked off, whole machine + target + a worker in frame, 30-120 s (do not stop recording)
   - G3 machine-height tracking shot, lens 10-30 cm above the slab, 15 s
   - G4 low angle up the machine or the built wall with a person beside it, 8 s
   - G5 the plan: BIM on a laptop, then the same view on the tablet / controller, 6 s each (also screen-record it)
   - G6 the result close-up: printed line, drilled hole, placed pile, top-down, 4 s
   - G7 inserts: gloved hands, tool tip, dust, track, status light, 6-8 shots of 2-3 s
   - G8 safety: tape, warning sign, E-stop pressed by a worker, 3 shots of 3 s
   - G9 the "before": the same task done by hand (snapping lines, overhead drilling), same angle as G2, 10 s
   - G10 interviews: superintendent and customer in PPE, medium close-up on thirds, site behind, 2-3 answers each with one story and one number; plus 30 s of room tone
   - G11 crew walking to camera or a team shot, 10 s
3. **Stock only for context** (city skyline, the industry problem), never for proof. 1080p minimum, 4K preferred, same frame rate, no AI clips, no other brands' logos, licence logged. Queries: `construction site aerial drone 4k`, `construction worker hard hat sunrise`, `concrete pour slab top down`, `tower crane city skyline golden hour`, `worker drilling ceiling dust`, `building site timelapse`, `surveyor total station tripod`.
4. **Cut.** Case study 2-3 min at ASL 2.5-5 s: interview in 3-6 s bites with 2-4 s b-roll between sentences. Launch 60-90 s at ASL 1.7-2.3 s on 2 s bars. Hold G2 for 8-11 s at least once.
5. **Build the graphics (HTML/GSAP, Hyperframes).**
   - Field super: `.super{position:absolute;left:4vw;bottom:9vh;font:300 7vh/1 Oswald;text-transform:uppercase;color:#fff}` + role `font:400 2.7vh Oswald; letter-spacing:.06em` on `border-top:2px solid var(--brand)`; `tl.from('.super',{clipPath:'inset(0 100% 0 0)',duration:.4,ease:'power2.out'})`, hold 4.5 s.
   - Claim caption: `font:700 5.4vh Inter; left:50%; top:86vh; transform:translate(-50%,-100%)`, fade 0.25 s on the bar, hold 4-6 s.
   - Spec card: `background:rgba(255,255,255,.22); backdrop-filter:blur(10px)`; tag `1.4vh 'IBM Plex Mono'` with 1 px border; value `font:300 5vh Inter`; `tl.from(card,{autoAlpha:0,x:-20,duration:.4})`; hold 3-5 s.
   - Location label: `font:600 7vh 'Source Sans 3'; letter-spacing:.15em; left:10vw; top:85vh`, fade 0.5 s.
   - Timelapse tag: `font:500 1.9vh Inter; top:4vh; right:4vw; background:rgba(0,0,0,.55); padding:.6vh 1vw` reading "TIMELAPSE 20×".
   - Logo: on white for a contractor feel, white on black for tech; 3-6 s; 0.3-0.6 s silence before it.
6. **QC.** Every worker in PPE; a scale cue in every big shot; every claim has a name or a shot; renders and timelapses labelled; -14 LUFS, true peak below -1 dBTP.

## Variants
**Tool / product launch (Hilti Jaibot + Nuron, Dusty FieldPrinter 2, Built Exosystem).** 60-150 s, ASL 1.7-2.3 s, music-led, -13 to -20 LUFS. The pain shot first (worker drilling overhead in dust), 2.5 s black + 1 s silence, reveal on the hit. Claims as 3-5-word captions (≈5.4 % H) or a keyword pattern in big caps (≈12 % H) held over 2-6 shots. Product shot at its own height; name in 3D on the slab or filmed on the machine. One spec at ≈80 %. Logo in silence. Add what all four lack: one full real-speed cycle and a named site.

**Site case study (Dusty × DPR, Liebherr × SMC Marine, Bechtel PJET, Built RPD 35).** 1.5-3.7 min, ASL 2.5-5.2 s, 0-5 % shots under 1 s, -21 to -14 LUFS. 3-5 named customer people in PPE, one checkable anecdote, real UI then real action, total station or cab screen in frame, location labels for each site, co-brand cards. Add burned-in captions and one number card per spoken figure (most references have neither).

**Megaproject / project documentary (ICON Wolf Ranch + Multi-story robot, Bechtel Built by the Bold).** 4-11 min, ASL 3.1-4.7 s (anthem cuts 1.6 s). Open with outside proof (press coverage used with permission, or headlines as document shots; a partner executive), a big chapter title with the speaker in front, two-camera interviews in the built space, drone grids that show how many, crew on ladders next to walls, residents or users at the end, a 15-17 s final aerial. Black holds of 1-2 s or a 1.25-2.5 s pause between chapters. For a company without archive: problem today → what we build → the people, team walking to camera in one take.

**Startup robot-on-site demo (Built Physical AI, Dusty FieldPrinter 2, with `industrial-robotics.md` for proof takes).** 60-75 s, ASL ≈2.2 s, even 4-6 cuts per 10 s, -14 LUFS. One worker's day as the spine: arrive in the truck → set the safety zone (tape, sign) → load the plan on the laptop → robots start → one person supervises several machines (split screen) → the human tasks that remain → data of the result → dusk wrap → logo on black. Shoot it with the one-camera + drone list above in one day; keep the full G2 cycle as a 30-60 s companion clip for buyers who want proof. Label any visualisation layered on real footage ("Visualisation") and any sped-up shot.

**Brand / recruiting anthem (Bechtel 2023 + 2026, Liebherr One Passion + Bauma, Hilti Life at HQ).** 1.5-4 min, ASL 1.0-2.2 s, letterbox optional. Relay of employees (office and field in PPE), one line each, burned-in captions ≈2.9 % H; or one inner-voice VO with huge value words (≈13 % H) on the cuts; sunrise grade; 6-10 drone shots; white end card with claim → URL → logo.
