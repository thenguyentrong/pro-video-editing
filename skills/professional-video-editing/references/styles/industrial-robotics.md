## Signature (one paragraph)
Credibility is the product. The proof is never a montage: the machine works in long takes (median 4.5-7.5 s) or in one uncut take of up to 158 s, full body plus workpiece plus target in frame, at real speed, in a real place with real clutter and real people. Every edit that changes time is admitted with a small corner label ("Autonomous", "2X"). Right before (or while) the machine acts, the film shows what the machine sees: its camera inset, a segmentation mask, a point cloud, or the real software UI with its decision. Numbers come as datasheet cards: one spec per shot, a big value with its unit, one accent colour, a thin leader line that touches the real object. Real footage and CGI are never mixed in one shot: a spec film is all render, a demo is all real, a brand film puts a clearly stylised vision after the real material. Claims are spoken or signed by named people with full titles at named sites.

Market reality (20 more humanoid and quadruped films measured, 2023-2026): most makers now skip the labels. Only 2 of the 20 carry a speed label (both Unitree), only 1 labels autonomy per step and shows teleoperation on screen (1X launch film), and 2 write "real footage / not AI" on screen (Unitree, AgiBot). So a small honest label is still the cheapest way to look more credible than the market.

Classic automation (‡ 17 arm, cobot and factory films, 2016-2026, KUKA, FANUC, Universal Robots, Festo, Bosch Rexroth, TRUMPF): none carries a speed or autonomy label; 4 are 100 % CGI and 4 more use CGI or a flat animation for specs or hidden parts; only 4 show a product number as a graphic; 4 keep a real take of 20 s or longer (23.8-≈163 s). Their strength is brand discipline: one brand colour runs through robot, graphics and end card.

## Structure
Demo-led product film (the default). Beats with share of runtime:
1. **Hook (0-6 %).** The machine already working in its real place, wide, full body and task readable. No logo first.
2. **Context (6-20 %).** Where and who: a named site label, or a named person with title, or a short version history (previous prototypes dim on black).
3. **Capability blocks (20-80 %), 3-6 times.** Each block: one plain claim (caption or spoken) → the machine's view (inset, mask, map, UI) → the real action in one long take with its speed label.
4. **Stress test (inside the blocks, ≈40-60 %, or late at ≈83-88 %).** A person moves the parts, an unknown object chosen on camera, a fallen part set upright, a self-correction close-up.
5. **Proof wide or hero (80-92 %).** The longest real take (Tesla: one 22.6 s shot with a crowd), or a slow 8-10 s push-in on the real machine.
6. **Logo (last 4-10 %).** 3-5 s on black or a dark brand gradient. Optional light-hearted 8 s extra after the logo.

Brand-story order (Boston Dynamics): people/dream (≈9 %) → archive with original watermarks (≈16 %) → today's real deployments → the product, real → stylised CGI vision (≤≈10 %) → real engineers' hands → real hero shot → logo.

## Picture
- **Framing:** medium-wide, eye level, whole machine + workpiece + target. Close-ups only as inserts (gripper, tool tip, status LED). Locked-off tripod or slow gimbal follow at 2-4 m with a ≈24 mm-equivalent lens.
- **Speed:** real time by default. No slow motion, no speed ramps on proof footage. Any speed-up gets a label in the same frame.
- **Grade:** neutral, natural site light. Option (Figure): near-monochrome `saturate(.3-.4)` and keep one brand colour from the site. Never a stylised grade on proof.
- **Show the machine's mind:** robot camera as a picture-in-picture (≈33 % wide, left) with a semi-transparent mask on the grasped part; robot POV in a rounded inset (≈75 % wide, centred on black) with a virtual model snapped onto the real arm; round-masked mini-map (≈25 % wide, bottom-right); full-frame point cloud; 2/3 real footage + 1/3 grey panel with the sensor view and tracked markers; real software UI on a monitor, then full screen for 3-4 s right before the machine moves. One cool accent colour for all overlays (cyan, blue or green).
- **More machine-mind formats (†):** split screen, 61 % third-person view + 39 % grid of four robot cameras and live logs, in one uncut 208.8 s take (Unitree); the real operator app with its mode field (MANUAL → AUTO), a digital twin and a third-person inset, as one 88.6 s screen recording that ends on the inspection result (ANYbotics); a height-map point cloud laid over a real stair climb; the robot's own chest display filmed in macro ("Picking" + progress %, Apptronik).
- **Teleoperation, shown honestly (†1X):** a status pill ("Expert Mode Enabled") plus a colour change on the robot (head ring turns blue), and the remote operator seen on a phone. Every other new film leaves teleop unstated, and Unitree posts teleop demos on the same channel as its stunts.
- **Real world:** keep clutter, cables, other people working, forklifts. Fleet shots (several machines in one frame) suggest scale.
- **Real vs CGI:** never in the same shot. Digital twins are drawn as clearly stylised wireframes, then cut or cross-fade to the real object from the same angle. Counter-examples (†): Agility's launch composites a CGI hologram robot into real footage without saying so; AgiBot's near-photoreal simulation is told apart from real takes only by a ≈4.5 % corner tag. Both weaken trust: if simulation must appear, give it a different look (grid floor, flat light) and a big "Simulation" tag.

Rules: no icons, no boxes on specs, units always written ("25 kg", "3.5 t"), metric first. Say autonomy and speed every time it applies. When speed honesty is the selling point (a stunt, a fast run), write it as a sentence at ≈5 %; when it is a footnote, a ≈2 % corner badge.

## Transitions
- Hard cuts. None inside a proof take.
- Data views, split screens and UI recordings enter as their own shots, or as a PiP that fades 0.3 s or scales in 0.35 s `back.out(1.4)`.
- Wireframe twin: draw 1.2 s `power1.inOut` (stagger 0.03 per path), hold, cross-fade 0.4 s to real footage from the same angle.
- Cross-dissolves of ≈0.5 s only inside b-roll blocks of interviews and walk-throughs.
- Fade (or fade to white 0.4 s) into the logo.
- † Launch films add white-flash and whip cuts (Digit 5) or 1-2 frame flash frames (D1 Max); never inside proof. Brand-colour full-frame cards as chapter stings (ANYbotics coral) and a light reveal done in camera (Hello Apollo) also work.

## Sound
- **Proof take:** raw room tone, contact transients, motor whine; no music or a very low pad. Measured: -29.9 LUFS integrated.
- **Music-led demo:** upbeat electronic, cut on bars, not beats: 2 bars per shot (≈7.5 s at ≈128 BPM) or 2 beats (1.2 s at 100 BPM) with section changes on the bar hit every 4.8 s.
- **Spec film:** gated staccato music; 0.15-0.8 s silence before each number, then a click or hit as it appears.
- **Interviews:** lavalier dialogue over a quiet corporate bed; short music gaps (0.15-0.9 s) mark b-roll entries.
- **Logo sting:** after 0.2-0.65 s of silence (Siemens, measured rises +35 to +42 dB). A brand film puts a 0.25 s gap before its last spoken line instead (+25.5 dB hit after it). † Quadruped spec film: 1.15 s silence, then the logo hit.
- **† Chinese launch films** (Unitree, AgiBot) are music-only and very loud (-11.1 to -6.9 LUFS); Unitree ends on a safety-disclaimer card, not a call to action. US/EU launch films sit lower (-20.9 to -14.4 LUFS) and use voice: 1X's launch film has voice in ≈90 % of its runtime, Agility's Digit 5 almost wall to wall.
- **† Master check:** four new uploads peak above 0 dBTP (+0.1 to +4.8 dBTP, clipped). Do not copy that: master to -14 LUFS and -1 dBTP for web.

## Do / Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Show each capability in one long take (4.5-7.5 s) or one uncut take | Montage the proof into 1 s shots |
| Label every speed change small in a corner ("2X", "8X") | Speed up or slow down silently |
| Show the machine's view or the real UI right before it acts | Explain "how it works" only with words |
| One spec per shot, big value + unit, one accent colour, leader line to the object | Stack several numbers on one card or use icons |
| Keep real footage and CGI in separate shots or separate films | Composite render parts into proof footage |
| Let named people with titles make the claims, at a named site | Use anonymous voice-over for big claims |
| Include a disturbance or an unknown object | Show only perfect, lucky runs |
| Keep real clutter and real people in frame | Clean the set until it looks like a render |
| Show teleoperation on screen when it is used (mode pill, operator in frame) | Leave autonomy or teleop to the video description |
| Say "no speed-up" once at the start and re-label only the part that is sped up | Write "real footage, not AI" while ramps stay unlabelled |
| Keep proof in one take even in a launch film, or publish it next to it as a raw one-take clip | Let a 1.2 s montage be the only proof |
| ‡ Put a physical reference in each spec shot (floor ruler, target circle under the load) | Float a number in empty space with nothing to compare it to |
| ‡ Name the customer early: fact box or bar with company, place, staff, industry (FANUC 0-6 s, TRUMPF ≈60 s) | Leave who the customer is to the upload title |
| ‡ Burn in subtitles for every non-English or noisy-floor interview | Publish an English-titled case with untranslated speech (TRUMPF) |
| ‡ Put one process number (cycle time, rate, payback) on screen in every case film | Let "high rate" or "ROI" live only in speech and the title (FANUC, UR) |
| ‡ Keep the machine's own sound under the music in a one-take demo | Replace it fully with loud music (-11.3 LUFS, TRUMPF) - the viewer loses the real-time cue |

## Recipe
1. **Write one claim per capability** (3-6 claims). Each claim needs: one long real take, one machine-view asset, and if possible one number.
2. **Shot list for one camera** (phone or mirrorless + gimbal + tripod; lock exposure and white balance; one frame rate, 25p or 24p; record clean audio):
   - 01 establishing wide of the real site, gimbal walk-in, 6 s
   - 02 locked-off medium-wide, whole machine + workpiece + target, one full task cycle, 30-150 s, do not stop recording
   - 03 slow walk-around or rising orbit of the machine, 30-45 s
   - 04 tracking shot of the machine moving on site, 20-30 s
   - 05 disturbance: a person moves the part or brings an unknown object, 8-11 s
   - 06 correction close-up at the tool, 8-10 s
   - 07 inserts: tool tip, gripper, sensor, status LED, 4-6 shots of 2-4 s, slow slider moves
   - 08 screen recording (OBS) of the software and the sensor feed during shot 02
   - 09 named person (site manager, customer, engineer) medium-close, machine behind, lavalier, 2-3 short answers
   - 10 engineers' hands working on the machine, 3-4 shots
   - 11 hero: the machine moves towards camera, 8-10 s
   - 12 30 s of room tone and a close mic on the machine
3. **Stock only for context, never for proof.** At least 1080p, prefer 4K, same frame rate, no AI clips, no visible third-party logos, licence log per clip. Queries: `construction site crane aerial`, `robot arm factory close up`, `hand pressing emergency stop button`, `warehouse aisle forklift`, `factory workers assembly line`, `city street people walking 4k`.
4. **Build the labels.** Autonomy: `position:absolute; right:6vw; bottom:6vh; font:600 2vh Inter; color:#fff; text-shadow:0 0 8px rgba(0,0,0,.5)`. Badge: `top:4vh; right:4vw; padding:.6vh 1vw; background:rgba(0,0,0,.55); font:500 1.9vh Inter; letter-spacing:.1em`; `tl.set(badge, {textContent:'AUTONOMOUS 4X'}, t)` with `video.playbackRate = 4` at the same `t`.
5. **Machine view.** PiP `<video>`: `left:0; top:15vh; width:33vw`, mask layer `#5b4dff` at opacity .6, fade 0.3 s when the step starts. UI proof: cut the real recording full screen for 3-4 s right before the action. Never fake a UI.
6. **Spec card.** `<div class="spec"><span class="lbl">[payload]</span><span class="val">25 kg</span><i></i><span class="alt">55 lbs</span></div>`, Montserrat, sizes from the Type table; leader `<line>` + `<circle r=4>` in the one accent colour, `stroke-width:2`, keyframe the object point every 4-6 frames. Play a click after a 0.2-0.4 s music cut.
7. **Cut.** Proof shots 4-8 s (or uncut), captions = shots, one block per claim. Hero 8-10 s with `gsap.fromTo(video, {scale:1}, {scale:1.08, duration:9, ease:'none'})`. Logo 3-5 s on black.
8. **QC.** No proof shot under 3 s; every speed change labelled; every teleoperated shot labelled; every machine-view asset is real; no shot mixes render and real; every claim has a name, title or site; true peak below -1 dBTP.

## Variants
**Proof demo (Boston Dynamics, Tesla, Siemens).** One take or 6-14 long shots, captions or dialogue, speed badges, machine view per capability. Siemens adds a story: a visitor asks, an engineer answers, the visitor brings a test object, the UI confirms, the machine acts in one locked-off shot. † Model test (Unitree WVLA): one uncut 3:29 split-screen take, a person moves the bin mid-task; add the "1x · autonomous" label it lacks. † Skills catalogue (Agility, 1X Redwood): one 10.6-13.6 s take per skill, a skill title (≈9 % caps) or a small lower-case word ("unseen object", "retrial") beside the action, a failed grasp and retry kept in, a final overhead of the whole cell with dashed callouts to each station.

**Spec film (BD Atlas features, Figure 02, Optimus Gen 2).** BD: 100 % CGI, one spec per shot of 2.5-6.5 s, datasheet cards, yellow tracked graphics, staccato hits. Figure: real robot in 27-43 s continuous camera moves with typewriter spec labels, then a named customer plant. Tesla: real footage, one measurable claim per 4-8 s shot, live sensor HUD keyed to real contacts.

**Launch event (ABB F712).** Named people from president to UX designer say the claims; the key phrase appears as wide-caps kinetic type at the same moment; SLAM point view and real software; giant product number with the real machine composited in front. Pair stunt energy with at least one long real-speed work shot.

**Customer case / testimonial (Siemens × Audi, ABB × Schunk).** The customer says the claim. Trading-card name intro (cut-out in grayscale on navy, name in gradient caps ≈9 %, 1.7 s) or small supers; talk shots 4-14 s; b-roll bursts of 3-6 shots every 10-30 s of talk; product working behind the speakers. † First day at a customer (Agility × GXO): the robot in the customer's colours, a permanent site label (≈4 %), full task-cycle takes of ≈15 s including one overhead, co-founder audio under b-roll, ASL rising from 3.5 s to 9.4 s as the film calms down. † Service-industry case (AgiBot hotel): the manager on camera with the robot working behind her, the interviewer's question as a text card, every line subtitled.

**Deployment montage (Figure F.03 at BMW).** 30-35 real-site clips on a 2-beat grid (1.2 s at 100 BPM), fleet shots, named site in the title. Weak as proof: it hides task duration and shows no autonomy label. Add both if you use it.

**Day-one startup reel (ABB × CyBe).** What a small team can film itself in one visit: a phone selfie walk-and-talk intro (8-10 s), 4-5 handheld walk-through shots of 6-10 s, one long 10 s shot of the machine working, one close pass along the finished product, both logos as constant corner bugs (own logo in a white box ≈10 % high bottom-left, partner logo bottom-right at opacity .85), 0.5 s dissolves, end card on white. ASL ≈6.2 s, -17.5 LUFS. Lift it with what the original lacks: burned-in captions (Inter 600, 3.5vh, white on a 60 % black pill, bottom centre), one data card over the long machine shot, a speed label if sped up, and a 3-frame white flash into the end card.

**† Humanoid launch film (Unitree H2, AgiBot A3, Apptronik Hello Apollo, 1X NEO Gamma + NEO launch, Agility Digit 5).** 0:37-2:34 for the film itself (1X also made a 9:53 keynote-style version). Measured: ASL 1.21-3.84 s, median 0.98-2.58 s, 0-41 % shots < 1 s, -20.9 to -6.9 LUFS. Beats: (1) greeting or human context 0-18 % (a "hello" typed like a chat message, or city crowds and workers with no robot yet); (2) reveal 15-40 % (the robot walks out of the dark as the light comes up, or a 3 s rack focus, Digit 5 puts its biggest loudness rise, +26.4 dB, on this reveal; Hello Apollo puts its +30.9 dB hit on the first typed letter); (3) the robot among people 30-85 % (hand-overs, a wave, carrying boxes or laundry, 2-4 s shots); (4) vision or production 70-93 % (fleet, factory line, hands assembling); (5) logo or end line, last 5-10 %. Two market schools:
- **Chinese spectacle (Unitree, AgiBot):** dance, runway, flips, a kung-fu short film in ≈2.4:1 with actors and English subtitles; a thumbnail title frame for the first 0.32 s; music only and very loud; a permanent "real footage, no CGI" line (≈2.5 %) or a big speed sentence (≈5.5 %); a safety-disclaimer end card; no speed or autonomy label on the stunts themselves. The price sits in the upload title, never in the picture.
- **US/EU job or home (1X, Agility, Apptronik):** people in almost every frame, a warm or single brand colour (oat, teal), named customer or a customer logo on set, a price end card (1X), no stunts, and almost no labels. Proof is kept apart as a raw one-take clip (Apptronik tote picking, one 48.2 s locked-off take with three task cycles about 15.5 s apart; 1X Redwood takes of 12.7-13.6 s).
- **Build it as:** the US/EU structure plus the Chinese honesty line, and one real-speed take of 8-15 s inside the film. Keynote length (1X): chapter cards, interviews of ≈10 s shots, robot-view chores at ≈2.3 s ASL with task pills, then an autonomy chapter in one locked room with "Autonomous" over each sub-step, and teleop shown as a mode pill.

**† Quadruped field film (Unitree A2, AgiBot D1 Max, ANYbotics Rapid Response, Superior Mobility, Aker BP).** Two schools again.
- **Stunt-spec (Unitree, AgiBot), 2:07-2:10.** Hook stunt 0-8 % (through a glass pane, racing cyclists) with "No speed-up in this video" top-left; hero title on a dark sweep (≈8-10 %); spec blocks 10-85 %, one number per block written `Label: value UNIT` (≈5-6 %) or value ≈6 % over a ≈3.5 % line, with a dotted arrow to the robot: top speed, climb height, stairs, slope angle, IP rating in a river, payload with a person standing on the robot; LiDAR point cloud in one accent colour over real footage; endurance block 85-96 % as one 14.2 s time-lapse with a route-map inset, a live km counter, "Only this part 100x speed-up", ending on a duration + distance card; logo after a 1.15 s silence. Unitree cuts long (ASL 5.52 s, 4 % < 1 s); AgiBot cuts very fast (ASL 1.23 s, 50 % < 1 s) and leaves ramps unlabelled, so take Unitree's rhythm.
- **Inspection field (ANYbotics), 1:29-2:04.** Real plants, offshore platforms, night, rain and staged fog; the robot is the only saturated colour; brand-colour full-frame cards for title and end. Proof is the robot's data: height map over stairs, lidar map, gauge reading with a tracked circle and a tick, thermal min/max, and the real operator app (MANUAL vs AUTO) as one long screen recording. Named experts or customers sign the claims; a site-log title ("POC, site, year" ≈6.5 % + "DAY 3" ≈10 %); a co-brand end card. Measured: ASL 2.55-3.26 s for edited films (flat 2-6 cuts per 10 s), 20.65 s for the site log; -19.0 to -13.5 LUFS.
- **Shot list to add:** door exit and stairs, rain or night pass, gauge close-up, the UI recording during the run, one payload or person-on-robot test, one endurance time-lapse with its speed factor written on it.

- **Brand colour as the system:** FANUC yellow (robots, cards, rulers, target circles), KUKA orange (only robots, data light, logo), UR blues (squares and quarter circles, or a deep-blue field), Rexroth navy/cyan/red stripe bars, TRUMPF electric blue panels and tints, Festo blue line from the frame edge. Pick one colour and let it do supers, wipes and the end card.
- **Case-film beats (≈ share of runtime):** title over the real site 0-3 % (panel with a 2-5 line summary, or a five-line caps title + fact bar: company, industry, country, employees) → company and people 3-20 % → challenge 20-30 % (yellow box "The Challenge:", or an interviewer question on a navy card) → solution 30-75 % (b-roll grid, UI on a monitor, an explainer for hidden parts: CGI x-ray, flat beam diagram, white clay model) → result and people 75-95 % (one long process take, the team) → brand card 3-7 s, often over 3-7 s of silence.
- **Spec shot (FANUC):** the real machine, one value per shot, a box in the brand colour top-left (≈4.5 % H, 2 lines) and a physical reference in frame (white floor ruler with ticks, a target circle under the load). Statement cards between specs: two lines ≈5 % H, line 2 in the brand colour, over a macro darkened ≈60 %.
- **Kinetic statements (UR Gen 7):** heavy condensed type ≈14 % H per line, 3-4 lines, words appear one by one (0.15-0.3 s), first words in the light brand colour, last words white; between them 5-13 s product orbits with leader-line callouts (1 px line, small circle on the part, pill ≈1.8 % H).
- **Application catalogue (Rexroth, silent):** split screen, 45 % text panel (category bar + three stepped tags) and 55 % render with the product glowing cyan; 8 blocks of 10-12 s; one key-data card with "up to" limits at the end. Good for stand screens; add VO and music for the web.
- **One-take demo (FANUC cell, TRUMPF vertical):** tripod, machine + load + output in one frame, 20-30 s per cycle or one ≈163 s take; music only. Add the missing cycle timer, "Real time" label and machine sound.
- **Gaps to beat:** 0 of 17 label speed or autonomy; only 4 show a product number as a graphic; the payback (UR "1,500 hours") and "high rate" (FANUC) claims are never on screen; one English-titled case keeps German speech without subtitles; all four CGI launches show no real machine.
- **Build it as:** the FANUC spec shot (real machine + physical reference) + the case-film beats with a fact box and subtitles + one 20-30 s locked cycle with a timer + one brand colour for every graphic.
