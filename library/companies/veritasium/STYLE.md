## In one line
A team of hosts tests a science question for real: a cinematic cold open (CG recreation, illustrated comic or a stunt) starts the story on frame 1, then the film alternates short live-action proof (hosts, labs, experts, archive) with long, uncut explanatory animations of 20–40 s.

## Sub-styles
1. **History + mechanism** (`fiber-optics`): photoreal 3D CG period recreation as the cold open, then the team re-does the old experiment, archive with a phrase-by-phrase quote card and big serif numbers, 2D cel-shaded character animation for historical people, and flat cream/teal diagrams of 15–40 s for the mechanism. Desk host on a living-room set.
2. **Stunt + field test** (`spider-silk`): the founder-host promises a physical stunt in the first 3 s, a countdown and a cut to black hold back the result; a second host narrates from a home set; field trip to a university lab, the measurement shown live and then rebuilt as a navy graph with one pink curve; value captions with physical comparisons.
3. **Investigation** (`gps-jamming`): the mystery is told as a hand-drawn comic (one 18 s shot), then photoreal 3D (antenna, Earth, orbits), dark grid charts, drone shots with place labels, researcher webcam interviews, and the host outdoors linking the evidence. Its own techno typeface for all labels.

## Measured numbers (first 300 s each)
- ASL 3.0–5.3 s, median 1.8–3.1 s; 0–16 % of shots under 1 s, 20–53 % over 3 s; 11–20 shots per minute.
- Two speeds: live action and archive at 1–3 s per shot (bursts of 6–9 cuts per 10 s), and one long animation per idea of 15–40 s (longest 31.6–39.5 s in every film). Stretches of 20–30 s with zero cuts are normal.
- Front-loaded: the first two quarters are the fastest (ASL 1.7–5.0 s and 2.3–2.9 s), the third and fourth slow down to 5.4–9.4 s as the mechanism starts.
- Fastest moment: a screen burst of 4 cuts at 0.25–0.33 s (fiber, 117 s); countdown jump cuts of 0.54 s (spider, 12–13 s).
- Cuts follow speech, not music: cut-on-onset 52–54 % vs chance 55–59 %.
- Loud, dense mix: −13.3 to −16.3 LUFS integrated, LRA 3.9–5.1 LU; 50 ms RMS median −20…−24 dBFS, p10 −37…−40 dBFS (bed ≈16–17 dB under the voice), only 2.5–3.5 % of windows below −45 dBFS. True peaks −1.0 to +0.2 dBTP (two of three above −1).
- Drop-outs 13–30 per 300 s, 0.3–1.15 s, to −45…−68 dBFS, with re-entry rises of +16…+49 dB.

## Hook (first 30 s)
Measured in all three films:
- **Frame 1 is story, not branding.** Voice from 0.0 s in all three; no logo, no title card, no channel intro, no sponsor (sponsor reads sit at ≈14–16 min or at the end).
- **Open on the inciting moment, told in a heightened visual register**: photoreal CG period scene (fiber), a stunt promise to camera (spider), a hand-drawn comic recreation (gps). Two of three show no host face before 26–32 s.
- **One long shot inside the hook**: CG 8.6 s ×2 (fiber), the comic 18.35 s (gps); the stunt open is the exception with 8 cuts in 10 s.
- **A near-silence beat at ≈15–18 s** in all three: −29…−31 dBFS at 15–17.5 s (fiber, slow-motion bead), −36…−38 dBFS at 16–17.5 s (spider, black content-warning card), −52 dBFS at 17.45 s (gps, glitch out of the comic). It separates the cold open from the "real world" part.
- **Switch to the real world at 17–32 s**: second host at 17.6 s (spider), 3D antenna at 18.35 s and host at 26.1 s (gps), the team's real recreation at 32.1 s (fiber).
- **Open loop**: the result is withheld (cut to black before the swing; "what was in the data"; "what the fibre became"). The explicit question or test is stated later, at ≈38–60 s.
- Cuts in 0–30 s: 4 / 7 / 17 (gps / fiber / spider).

Template (30 s): 0–15 s cold open in one heightened register (CG, illustration or stunt), voice on frame 1, 1–3 long shots → 15–18 s dip to −35 dBFS or lower for 1–2.5 s → 17–30 s first real-world shot (host, place or data) with a lower third → question/test stated by 40–60 s.

## Type & graphics
- **One type system per film**, matched to the subject: editorial serif (fiber, spider), wide techno sans (gps), comic lettering for illustrated sequences. There is no fixed brand template.
- Lower third (same layout in every film): name ≈3–3.3 % H, white, no box, bottom-left at x ≈3 % W; role in a smaller line ≈1.8–2 % H (italic serif + thin white rule in the serif films). Hosts and experts are labelled the first time they appear.
- Value captions: ≈6 % H serif, white with a soft shadow, centred low (≈88 % H) or beside the subject; big money numbers ≈8 % H.
- Place labels and credits: small (≈1.6–3 % H) bottom-left. Third-party footage always carries a credit line ("The Hunt - BBC", "AT&T Archives", "NASA").
- Quote card: light italic sans ≈5 % H, centred over darkened archive, revealed phrase by phrase (unread words grey).
- Graph/diagram grounds: navy `#040811` with one pink `#E0407A`≈ curve; cream `#FEF2C8` / teal `#083846` flat diagrams with orange waves; black with a faint grid for data charts; math in a LaTeX-style serif.
- Animation crafts: photoreal 3D CG, 2D cel-shaded characters, hand-drawn comic panels, flat vector diagrams, 3D Earth/orbits. The gps film states on screen that its art is made by people.

## Picture
- Hosts on clean, lived-in sets (living room with plants and a sofa, bookshelf, garden, brick wall), medium close-up, eye-level, natural light.
- Field footage is handheld documentary with the crew sometimes in frame; macro and microscope for detail.
- Third-party clips keep their own look and get a credit. Archive is black-and-white, lifted.
- No letterbox, no film grain; the cinematic grade is kept for the CG cold opens (warm amber, shafts of light).

## Rules
1. Start the voice on frame 1 with the story's inciting moment; no logo, no "hi", no sponsor in the first 30 s.
2. Make the cold open a different visual register (CG, illustration, or a stunt) and hold 1–3 long shots of 8–18 s, unless it is a stunt (then 6–8 cuts in 10 s).
3. Put a 1–2.5 s dip to −35 dBFS or lower at ≈15–18 s, then cut to the real world.
4. Withhold the payoff (cut to black, unanswered tip, unexplained object); state the test by 40–60 s.
5. Live action, archive and b-roll at 1–3 s per shot; give each mechanism one uncut animation of 15–40 s.
6. Show the measurement for real (machine, microscope, website, data file), then rebuild it as a clean graph.
7. Label every person and place the first time (name ≈3 % H, bottom-left); credit every borrowed clip.
8. One accent colour per graphic world; one typeface family per film.
9. Mix loud (−13…−16 LUFS) with a continuous bed ≈16 dB under the voice and short drops (0.3–1 s) at section breaks.

## Caveats
- Only the first 300 s of each film was measured; sponsor reads and later chapters are not covered.
- Cut counts were corrected by eye (dark CG footage; one continuous 18 s comic counted as one shot; glitch and white-flash frames folded). Animated graphics with internal changes count as one shot, so ASL for diagram-heavy stretches is high by design.
- Fonts are guesses: the serif reads like a Caslon-style text face (Libre Caslon Text / Source Serif 4 are free stand-ins); the techno face is close to Michroma.
- Hex colours marked ≈ are eyeballed from 480p frames.
- Music is described by level only (bed depth, drop-outs), not by genre; whether the bed stops at each drop-out was not checked by ear.
- Three different films, three different type systems: the "house style" is in structure, rhythm and sound, not in a graphic template.
