## Sub-styles
1. **Launch film** - named people (host, president, managers) say the claims; the key phrase appears as big wide-caps kinetic type at the same moment; club-lit warehouse, a stunt rider for energy; SLAM point-cloud view; product number as a giant title with the robot in front.
2. **Partner interview** - two speakers on the booth with the product behind them, gradient title card, small name supers, b-roll blocks of macro slider shots with dissolves.
3. **Customer visit reel** - selfie walk-and-talk, handheld b-roll, logo bugs of both companies, no captions.

## How ABB shows a real machine working
- Real product, real speed; motion energy comes from camera moves, light and a stunt rider - not from speed ramps.
- Macro slider shots of joints, status LEDs, gripper fingers (smallest-details); long locked shot of a printer at work (CyBe).
- Data is shown as a perception view (SLAM points, green corner brackets) and a software screen recording, not as numbers.

## Typography system (2026)
- Wide extended geometric caps (Lexend Zetta / Michroma look) for names and keywords; humanist sans for roles and small lines.
- Name super: NAME (caps ~4 %) + role (sentence case ~2.3 %), white, bottom-left, no box.
- Kinetic captions = the spoken phrase, typed with a cursor or red-to-white sweep, 2-4 words per line.
- Brand colour: ABB red; gradient blobs red/white/lilac on title cards; white end card with logo.

## Lessons for a construction-robot startup
- The CyBe reel is the realistic "day 1" format (phone, partner visit) - add captions, one data card and a proper end card to lift it.
- The launch format works when the claims are said by named people; repeat the spoken claim as on-screen type at the same moment.
- Don't let stunts replace proof: pair any "energy" b-roll with one perception/data view and one long real-speed work shot.
