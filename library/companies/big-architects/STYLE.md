## Core idea
An architecture film shows one idea, then proves it in space. The idea comes first, in the simplest form: a line diagram on black (box → unfolding net; boxes stacking), or CG boxes stacked into the real street behind the architect. Then the camera moves slowly through the building with people in every frame. The building's concept also becomes the film's form: a ride is filmed as one ride; a stacked tower ends as stacked boxes.

## Rules with numbers
- **Slow**: ASL ≥ 4 s; render moves 6–16 s; real-footage takes up to 40–57 s. Shots per minute 3.6–14.6.
- **Diagram on black** at the start (7 s) or the end (12 s): white 1–2 px lines or white solid boxes, nothing else.
- **People in every render** for scale and life; real crowds in real footage.
- **Text is rare**: film-style credits (role ≈3 % H over a logo) at the start and the end; no captions, no data, no lower thirds.
- **The project pattern is the transition**: halftone dot dissolve, façade-pattern wipe.
- **Sound**: one ambient or building track; voice only when the architect presents; long silences at the edges (0.8–1.5 s at the start, 0.8–3.4 s at the end).

## Recipe (a 2-minute project film)
1. 0–10 %: logos/credits on black or over context footage.
2. 10–20 %: the concept diagram (7–10 s), or the architect on site with the idea composited behind him.
3. 20–85 %: 6–10 slow moves (8–16 s each), outside → inside → roof, day → dusk → night, people everywhere.
4. 85–100 %: the building in its city (aerial or skyline), then a diagram or credit coda on black, end in silence.
