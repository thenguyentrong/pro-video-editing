## Sub-styles
1. **Brand anthem (2026).** Three acts with black holds as chapter breaks: archive photos in a vintage slide frame (≈3 s each) -> today's megaprojects (1-2 s drone shots) -> people; one-word slams fill the frame width; four employees speak to camera; pace rises from ASL 1.96 to 1.24 s per quarter, then one 24 s walk-to-camera take.
2. **Relay anthem (2023).** Seven employees (office + field in PPE) each say one line straight to camera; every line is a burned-in caption; adjective list one word per 1.0-1.3 s shot; quiet mix (-18.9 LUFS) with a 0.65 s drop before the last line.
3. **Project case study (2013).** Staff interviews on the real site with full name + title supers, location labels for each megaproject, material stacks for scale, dissolves, globe-logo bookends.

## Rules with numbers
- Megaproject scale comes from drone and top-down shots of plants, tanks, solar fields, motorways, ports; on the ground from walls of materials (pipe spools, cable drums, containers).
- People are the proof: every film has staff speaking to camera; field staff always in hard hat, hi-vis, safety glasses.
- One idea per shot: one caption line, one adjective, or one giant word.
- Chapter breaks: 1.1-1.9 s black holds (2026) or a drop-out of 0.6 s before the last line (2023, 2026).
- Logo end card on white (2023, 2026), 3-5 s, URL under it.

## Typography system
- Word slam: ultra-bold neo-grotesk caps (free: Inter 900 / Archivo Black), white, fitted to ≈90 % frame width (cap height 15-40 % H), image framed by a white border.
- Captions: humanist sans (Open Sans), sentence case, ≈2.9 % cap height, centred at y ≈86 %, shadow, no box.
- Supers (2013): name letter-spaced caps ≈5.5 % + role ≈3.5 %, right-aligned bottom-right; location label caps ≈7 % bottom-left.
- The 2026 anthem uses no supers at all; employees are unnamed.

## How Bechtel shows megaprojects credibly
- Name the project or the historic first (location labels, "VISIONARY" over the pipeline).
- Show the real workforce on the real site in PPE, plus office engineers - both sides of delivery.
- Pair every big claim with an aerial of the actual asset (LNG, nuclear, airport, chip fab).

## Lessons for a construction-tech startup
- A startup has no archive, but can copy the structure: problem today -> what we build -> the people (team walks to camera in one take).
- Relay narration by your own team + burned-in captions is cheap, credible and works with sound off.
- One-word slams over real site footage give a brand film rhythm without CGI.
- For a pilot-project film use the 2013 grammar, modernised: on-site interview with name + role super, location label, one number card per spoken figure.
