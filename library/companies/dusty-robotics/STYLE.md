## Sub-styles
1. **Launch film** - no voice; a 1 s stutter intro with black dips; the product name stands in 3D on the slab and the robot drives in front of it; one claim per caption (3-5 words, white bold, bottom centre) held 4-6 s over the shot that proves it (puddles, clutter, printed text, curves); spec at the end (1/16" accuracy).
2. **Customer case study** - 4-6 named site people (GC + trades) in full PPE, medium close-up on thirds, site behind; one checkable anecdote (an off-position pipe found early); b-roll of total station, tablet app, crew reading the floor; co-brand card at both ends.
3. **Workflow explainer** - flat isometric illustration in brand palette, requirements checklist first, then 5 steps with real tool names, ends on a QA/productivity report with numbers.

## Rules with numbers
- Camera at robot height: lens 5-30 cm above the slab for most launch shots; top-down only for printed output.
- Launch pace: ASL 2.3 s, cuts on 2 s bars (≈120 BPM), caption = claim, changes on a bar hit; 0.45 s drop-out before the final reveal; logo on white in silence.
- Case pace: ASL ≈5.2 s, 0 % shots under 1 s, cuts on sentences (cut-to-onset 25 % vs 53 % chance), b-roll 2-6 s, longest proof shot 10.8 s (robot + total station).
- Loudness -18 to -20 LUFS, low LRA (2.9-7.7 LU).
- Brand orange (≈#E8792B-#F07C1E) is the only saturated colour; grey concrete everywhere else.

## Typography system
- Launch captions: Inter Bold-like 700, white, sentence case, ≈5.4 % H cap height, centred at ≈86 % from top, no box.
- Product name: same face, brand orange, ≈7 % H, placed in perspective on the floor.
- Name supers (case): thin condensed caps (Oswald 300 / Bebas Neue), name ≈7 % H + role ≈2.7 % H on a 2 px orange rule with a tick, on the empty side of the frame.
- Explainer: Inter/Manrope, navy on pale aqua, orange kicker boxes, caps headline ≈7 % H.

## How they keep the site credible
- Real unfinished decks with puddles, shoring, pipe protectors, timber; real workers' boots and gloves (battery swap).
- The output is shown big: printed lines, room names, door notes, curves, in top-down close-ups.
- The real tool chain on screen: total station on a tripod, tablet app, crew using the printed floor.
- Customer staff with names and titles say the claims; Dusty itself only speaks in the explainer.
- Weak spots: launch film has no speed label (real-time assumed) and no numbers except accuracy; case study has no numbers or captions.

## Lessons for a construction-tech startup
- Shoot the small machine like a car: gimbal at floor level, wide lens, slow tracking - it costs nothing and lifts the whole film.
- Pair every claim caption with the one shot that proves it; never caption a shot that does not show the claim.
- For the case film, ask each site person for one concrete story with a time or cost; add burned-in captions and one number card (what DPR lacks).
- Explain setup requirements before features - buyers on site ask "what do I need?" first.
