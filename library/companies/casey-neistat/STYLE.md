All three are the most recent typical vlogs on the channel (June–August 2026); one later upload is a talk piece and was skipped as atypical.

## In one line
One man with a wide lens carries you through one day in the city: a self-shot wide opening, a quick world montage on music, a physical or bold title, then fisheye talk-to-camera in motion, drone and 360 shots for scale, and dry sit-down takes for the parts that need thinking.

## Sub-styles
1. **Event / adventure with sponsor** (`skate-tunnel`): a rare, time-limited event; the sponsor's 360 camera makes the best shots; sponsor shown on a handwritten sticky note; two dry desk takes explain the product. Slowest cut (median 4.5 s) because 360 reframing replaces cuts.
2. **Mission / experiment day** (`hottest-day`): one measurable question (a temperature), a number every 20–30 s, news-radio collage for context, one bold title card. Fastest and most even cut (median 2.6 s, ASL 3.0–3.4 s in every quarter).
3. **Road trip, day into night** (`leaving-nyc`): errands → drive → night → morning; road signs as location titles, dips to black and a windshield timelapse as time markers, long intimate talk takes (20 s, 34 s) at stops.

## Measured numbers (first 300 s each)
- ASL 3.2–6.5 s, median 2.6–4.5 s, 9–19 shots per minute; 2–21 % of shots under 1 s, 43–74 % over 3 s.
- Talk-to-camera takes are the longest shots: 11.7 s (walking), 20.1 s, 33.6 s and 34.7 s (sitting). Montage shots 0.4–1.5 s.
- Bursts: 6 cuts per 10 s in montages (road signs at 0.67–0.8 s; thermometer inserts at 1.2–1.5 s; timelapse at 0.4–1.8 s).
- Cuts land on audio onsets slightly above chance (43–47 % vs 38–40 %): montages are cut to the music, talk is cut on speech.
- Loud mix: −12.7 to −14.3 LUFS integrated, RMS median −17.5 to −19.8 dBFS, true peak above 0 dBTP on the re-encoded copies.
- Music bed under walking/montage sections (p10 only 2–9 dB under the median); sit-down and night talk is dry (pauses fall to −44…−57 dBFS).
- Designed silence: one 2.0 s digital silence under a "YESTERDAY" time card; otherwise no full drops.

## Hook (first 30 s)
Common pattern across the three films:
| beat | time | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Self-shot premise | 0 → 3.9–10.9 s | one locked-off wide (camera on the ground or a ledge) with the host doing the premise action: walking out with a board, cracking an egg on the street, skating from far into a close-up |
| 2. First line | 2.1–5.3 s | a short question, an invitation or a list — never a greeting to the audience, never "in this video" |
| 3. World montage on music | ≈8–25 s | 4–6 shots of 1.3–4.8 s (drone sunrise aerials, overhead 360 follow, skating); context from other voices (news radio) or none; host silent for 10–30 s |
| 4. Title moment | 22.4–28.9 s | a bold 3-line title card (1.5 s) OR a handwritten sponsor sticky note (4.6 s) OR a typed list built with the speech (3–13 s) |
| 5. Gear-up inserts | 24–34 s | 1.2–1.5 s inserts of the tool of the day (thermometer from a holster, a camera from its box), last one on the loudest music hit |
| 6. Premise said to camera | 43.5–52 s | the "what we are doing today" sentence comes only after the hook, in a fisheye selfie, often followed by a real sign as the title |
- Cuts in 0–30 s: 6, 11, 6. No channel logo, no intro sting, no "subscribe" in the hook.
- Sponsor (when there is one) at ≈29 s, as a physical card, then explained later in dry desk takes.

## Type
- Almost none. Titles are physical when possible: sticky notes, street signs, event banners, highway signs, a state welcome sign.
- When type is used: Helvetica-style grotesque (closest free: TeX Gyre Heros, or Inter).
  - Title card: bold caps, white, three lines justified to one width (≈60 % W), flush left at x ≈8 % W, lower half (≈51–87 % H), cap heights ≈9 / 12.5 / 10 % H, tight leading, 1.5 s, hard cut on/off.
  - List: bold sentence case, black with a soft white halo, left x ≈3.5 % W, top ≈33 % H, cap ≈6.7 % H, line pitch ≈10.4 % H, one line per spoken item.
  - Time card: regular caps, white, centred, cap ≈16.7 % H, over darkened blurred footage, ≈2 s, in silence.
  - Annotation: one flat white block arrow (≈26 % W) pointing at a reading, static.

## Picture & camera
- Wide and fisheye for almost everything; the host is close, in sunglasses, at arm's length, often moving (skateboard).
- 360 camera for third-person overhead follows and tiny-planet selfies; drone for sunrise/dusk/top-down; telephoto only for signs and the sun.
- Locked-off self wides open each film and return for big actions.
- Sit-down desk takes in his studio: wide lens at table height, props/products in the foreground, warm practical lights.
- Natural, saturated grade; night kept dark with sodium orange; no letterbox, no grain.

## Transitions
Hard cuts; whip-blur and hand-over-lens cuts (1–3 per 5 min); 0.5–0.8 s dips to black and a timelapse for time passing; a silent "YESTERDAY" card for a flashback.

## Rules
1. Open on one self-shot wide (4–11 s) of the premise action; first line by 5 s.
2. Then 10–20 s of music montage with no host talk; title or sponsor card at 22–29 s.
3. Say the premise to camera only at 44–52 s; let the pictures make the promise first.
4. Keep walking talk at 2–12 s per shot and sitting talk long (15–35 s, no cuts, no music).
5. Montages to the music at 0.4–1.5 s per shot, up to 6 cuts per 10 s.
6. Prefer physical titles (notes, signs) over graphics; if type, one bold Helvetica card.
7. Mix loud (−13 to −14 LUFS) with music under movement and dry voice when seated.
8. Mark time with dips to black, timelapse, or a silent time card.

## Caveats
- Only the first 300 s of each film was analysed.
- The default scene detector missed 8–15 cuts per film in fisheye/360 footage; final numbers use the adaptive detector plus verified scene-only cuts.
- Speech timings come from a local speech-to-text pass (±0.5 s); the voice-over itself is not transcribed here.
- Fonts are guesses (Helvetica-like); sizes measured on 480p frames.
- Music style not described; levels only. True peaks above 0 dBTP may come from the 480p re-encode.
