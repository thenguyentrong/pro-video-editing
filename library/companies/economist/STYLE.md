## In one line
The magazine's own editors argue at a red round table; the brand is the set, the evidence is one house-style chart or a few news photos held long, and there is no music. The older narrated format ("The Essentials") is the opposite pace: an editor-presenter on grey seamless, photoreal 3D metaphors, sourced news clips and sepia archive, with a music bed.

## Sub-styles
1. **Studio round-table debate** (all three): 2-4 editors at the red table, 3-4 locked cameras, long takes, cuts on speaker turns.
2. **Remote correspondent take**: a bureau editor on a webcam feed, full screen for 30-47 s in one take, with a lower third and then a location tag ("Berlin").
3. **House chart insert** (nvidia): one full-screen Economist chart, static, held ~12.6 s under the voice, then kept visible on the studio screen in the wide shot.
4. **Narrated designed explainer, "The Essentials"** (`interest-rates-inflation`, 2022): the paper's economics editor presents on a dark grey seamless (front + 90° profile camera); 3D metaphors on a white vignetted ground (an old TV that plays the news clips, spinning "%" signs, a plumbing machine with tanks labelled SAVINGS / PRICES and an INTEREST RATES gauge, held 14 s); 1940s sepia instructional film and a vintage cartoon; "Source: <outlet>" top right on every borrowed clip; red "The Economist" box bug top left; series title in big serif caps. ASL 3.19 s / median 2.40 s, -15.4 LUFS with a continuous bed ~11 dB under the voice; 2.5 s silence after the series title.

## How they show evidence
- **Very little, held very long.** In 150 s: 2-3 news photos (3-4 s each, slow push ~5%), one illustrative still (15.6 s), or one chart (12.6 s). Never a montage.
- **The house chart** is the main evidence tool: black background, Economist-red bars, red dot + bold serif title top-left ("Nvidia, quarterly revenue, $bn"), y-axis numbers on the RIGHT, thin grey gridlines, axis note, "Source: FactSet" bottom-left. It does not animate - it cuts in complete and holds.
- **The chart lives in the set**: the same chart appears on the big screen behind the table in the wide shot for another 15 s, so the data stays in view while people talk.
- **Numbers inside photos** count as evidence too (an election results board "18,5% / 44% / 9%").
- **Metaphor machines** (narrated format): a photoreal 3D system (tanks, pipes, gauges) that turns a mechanism into plumbing; the same machine returns later with a new tank ("PRICES").
- **News inside a prop**: borrowed TV clips play inside a 3D old TV set, with "Source: Sky News" top right.
- **People are the authority**: every speaker's title ends ", The Economist"; the remote expert gets a city tag ("Berlin") = "our person on the ground".
- News photos and archive clips carry no on-screen credit in these windows (unlike WSJ/NYT).

## Captions & lower thirds
- Lower third flush to the frame edge on the side the speaker is NOT looking at; ~42% W wide; top at ~74% H.
  - Black bar 9.6% H: white serif name (cap ~3.5% H) + Economist-red dot (~2.5% H).
  - Grey ~85% bar 6.5% H: white sans job title (cap ~2.3% H), sentence case, ends ", The Economist".
  - Holds 4-5 s; appears on each speaker's first long take.
- Location tag: black box flush top-right (x 85-100% W, y 3-11% H), white serif city + red dot, stays for the whole remote take.
- Bug: white circle with a serif "E", top-left, ~7% H, always on (also over charts).
- No subtitles or captions at all.
- Narrated format instead: boxless lower third (white bold serif name ~5 % H, sans role ~3.3 % H, italic masthead line) at x ≈ 6 %, bottom ~88 % H; white serif keyword captions ~5 % H; source label top right ~3 % H; red box logo top left ~10 % H.

## Graphics restraint & colour
- Palette = the set: cream walls, black shelving and table base, one brand red (~#E3120B) on the table top, mugs, mic flags and chart bars. Charts use the same black + red, so studio and data look like one product.
- No motion graphics, no maps, no kinetic type, no grain, no letterbox. One graphic system (lower third + tag + bug + chart).

## Voice & music mix
- Narrated format: a continuous bed ~11 dB under the voice (only 1 % of 50 ms windows below -45 dBFS), -15.4 LUFS integrated, 2.5 s drop after the series title, SFX on 3D moves.
- Debate format: dialogue only. No music bed under conversation or under the chart (gap floors -58 to -80 dBFS; in the Japan episode the room is gated to digital silence between phrases).
- Broadcast level: -22.3 to -22.6 LUFS integrated; momentary median -20 to -24 LUFS, flat; no hits, no drop-outs used as structure.

## Rules
1. Cut on speaker turns at a pause, not on beats; ASL 7.5-9 s, median 4.5-6 s; no shot under ~1.9 s; let a strong speaker run 20-47 s in one take.
2. Open with the host's question to camera + lower third (0-6 s), then 2 news photos of 3-4 s, then the wide establishing shot (by ~13-15 s).
3. Lower third: black 9.6% H + grey 6.5% H bars, serif name + red dot, sans title ending with the masthead, flush to the edge opposite the eyeline, hold 4-5 s.
4. Remote expert: full screen, one take of 30-45 s, lower third first, then a city tag top-right for the rest of the take.
5. One chart per ~2.5 min: house style (black, #E3120B bars, right-hand y-axis, source bottom-left), static, hold 10-13 s, then show it again on the set screen in the wide.
6. Photos: 3-4 s, slow push 1 -> 1.05 linear; an illustrative still may hold up to 15 s.
7. No music under talk or data; master around -22 LUFS (broadcast) - for social, raise to -16 LUFS but keep it voice-only.
8. Brand through the set (one red object, branded mic flags), not through animation.
9. Narrated format: one photoreal 3D metaphor per mechanism, held 8-14 s and reused; every borrowed clip framed in a prop or labelled "Source: …" top right; ASL ~3 s, bed ~11 dB under the voice.

## Caveats
- Three of the four examples are the same 2026 studio-debate series; the narrated format is covered by one 2022 film only ("The Essentials"), found via search because recent uploads are all debates.
- Typefaces are guesses: the name serif looks like the Economist's own serif (closest free Source Serif 4), the sans like Econ Sans/Milo (closest free Fira Sans).
- The "To Do: Buy Japanese Yen" desk still (japan-world-economy) looks like an illustration on a press photo; not verified.
- Lower-third animation was not visible at 1 fps; the slide-in in the recipe is a suggestion.
