## In one line
A named reporter anchors the film, but the screen belongs to evidence - satellite images, leaked documents, maps, the real object - every piece credited with source, place and date, coded in one teal accent, over a quiet continuous music bed.

## Sub-styles
1. **Investigative "WSJ Explains"** (megaproject): reporter at a desk on a dark set + in profile on black; the subject's own promo renders as "the promise", then leaked document, satellite before/after and the official's own interview as "the reality"; a serif chapter card ("WSJ EXPLAINS / What is next for Neom").
2. **Object show-and-tell** (north-korea-phone): reporter holds a real object; macro inserts with hands; zero graphics; a witness walks into frame and speaks with chunked subtitles.
3. **Map-led news explainer** (houthi-chokepoint): reporter standing in the newsroom; 3-4 returns to one satellite-texture map style, each a long continuous move adding one fact; war footage with provenance; a chart as the "so what".

## How they show evidence
- **Promise vs reality**: show the subject's own material first (official renders, launch video, propaganda footage), then the WSJ's proof (leaked spreadsheet with a teal highlighter, Planet Labs / Vantor satellite images before and after, the governor's own TV answer).
- **Provenance on everything**: third-party clips and satellite images get a top-left credit block - SOURCE in tiny tracked caps / place / date ("HOUTHI MEDIA OFFICE / Taiz Province, Yemen / Sept. 12"; "VANTOR / East-West Pipeline Pumping Station, Saudi Arabia / Sept. 13"). Propaganda logos and Arabic chyrons are left visible.
- **Documents**: crop to the line that matters, 3 crops getting closer (1.5-2 s each), a teal ellipse on the key word and a teal highlighter wiping across the key sentence.
- **Maps**: desaturated satellite texture, dark seas, crimson for the adversary, teal for everyone else, white labels and dashed white front lines, an orange glow for the threatened point; one continuous camera move per map shot (8-16 s).
- **Scale comparisons**: 170 km line copied onto Connecticut at the same scale (split screen); a 500 m wall next to the Empire State Building and the Eiffel Tower.
- **Pins on real footage**: a white square + line + label ("Perim Island") placed in a drone shot to tie map and place together.
- **The real object** (phone) filmed like a product film: macro lens, hands, shallow focus, the provenance sticker in close-up.
- **Charts** at the end of an argument: near-black, teal bars, white bold sans title + unit subtitle, y-axis on the LEFT, tracked-caps source line; built as a left-to-right time sweep.

## Captions & lower thirds
- Lower third (all three films): flush left at x 5% W, top ~61% H; name in black condensed serif on a white box (~8% H box, name cap ~3.3% H); job title in white sans on a ~70% black box (cap ~2.1% H). 2-4 words each ("WSJ Finance Reporter", "North Korean Defector"). Hard on/off, hold 2-4 s.
- Subtitles: white semibold sans, no box, faint shadow, centred, baseline ~87% H, cap ~2.5-2.9% H. Translations in one-line chunks of 2-5 words (1.5-3 s each); quoted speech max 2 lines; off-mic interviewer questions are subtitled too.
- Bug: serif "WSJ" top-right, ~4% H, always on.
- Chapter card: bold sans kicker "WSJ EXPLAINS" (~3.8% H caps) over a white serif question (~8% H cap), centred on footage darkened ~50%, ~4 s.

## Graphics restraint & colour
- One accent family: teal/cyan (~#2EC4C9 / #1FB5B5) for annotation lines, highlighters, ellipses, shipping lanes and chart bars. Crimson (~#C8102E) only as a territory fill for an actor; orange glow (~#E8601C) only for one threatened point. White for outlines and labels.
- Footage keeps its source look (no unifying grade); the reporter is lit low-key (dark set or blurred newsroom); in the object film the wardrobe and set are black/charcoal so the red phone is the only saturated object (a red retro TV echoes it).
- Zero decoration: no grain, no light leaks, no paper textures, no letterbox.

## Voice & music mix
- Integrated -19 to -24 LUFS (broadcast-style, voice-led); momentary median steady around -20 to -24 LUFS.
- A continuous low music bed under the voice (gap floors -43 to -64 dBFS, never digital silence).
- Structure through silence: 1-2 music drop-outs per 150 s (0.5-2.0 s, 12-20 LU down) right before a chapter card, a witness's entrance or the evidence section, followed by a +16 to +32 dB rise.
- Cuts are not beat-synced (on-onset rate equals chance).

## Rules
1. Put the reporter on screen every 20-40 s for 2-7 s; they are the guide, not the subject. Two setups (to camera + profile on black, or standing in the newsroom).
2. Cut footage at 1-3 s (median 2.2-2.5 s, ASL 2.8-3.6 s); give maps and 3D explanations the long shots (8-16 s, one continuous move each).
3. Show the promise first, then the proof: subject's own renders/propaganda -> leaked doc / satellite before-after / real object -> the official's own words.
4. Credit every third-party image top-left in three lines: SOURCE caps 1.9% H / place 3% H / date 3% H. Keep source logos.
5. One accent colour (teal) for all annotations and charts; crimson only for an adversary fill, orange only for the one threatened point.
6. Make numbers physical: overlay distances on a familiar region at the same scale; compare heights with famous buildings.
7. Subtitles: one line, 2-5 words per chunk for translations; 2.5-2.9% H white semibold, bottom 13%, no box.
8. Mark chapter turns with sound: drop the bed to near-silence for 0.5-2 s, then come back +16-32 dB on the new section; master around -22 LUFS (or -16 for social).

## Recipe: real footage + HTML/GSAP (Hyperframes)
Layer stack (bottom -> top): footage / satellite still -> SVG map or annotation layer -> labels/pins -> subtitle -> lower third -> credit block (top-left) -> "WSJ"-style bug (top-right).

Mix: voice about -20 LUFS short-term, bed 15 dB under it, 0.5-2 s drop before chapter turns, master -22 LUFS integrated (-16 for social).

## Caveats
- Typefaces are guesses: names look like WSJ's Escrow Condensed (closest free "Libre Caslon Condensed" / "Playfair Display" tight), sans like WSJ Retina (closest free "Inter" / "Libre Franklin").
- Hex colours were read off 480p frames; treat them as close, not exact.
- Whether the quiet bed in the phone film is music or room tone is not certain (gap floors suggest a bed).
- Lower-third and label animation timings are not visible at 1 fps; GSAP values are suggestions consistent with what the frames show.
