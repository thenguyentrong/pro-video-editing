Only one example: treat the rules below as one film's measurements, not a house style.

## Core idea
The event is the edit. A flight test is told in strict time order with cameras that are physically on or near the vehicle, the team's reactions as punctuation, and mission audio instead of a narrator. No claim is written on screen.

## Rules (measured on one film)
- **No text** during the film; only a two-line caps tease of the next flight at the end (≈2.5 % H).
- **Flat rhythm:** ASL 2.2 s, median 1.88 s, only 8 % under 1 s; 3-7 cuts per 10 s from start to end.
- **Alternation:** machine view (onboard, tower, tracker) and human view (control room, crowd) every 3-5 shots.
- **Sound:** real countdown and status call-outs over music; no drop-outs; button ending; -15.1 LUFS, LRA 11.1 LU.
- **Picture:** native camera colour and lens distortion kept (fish-eye onboard, compression); no unified grade.

## How it makes trust visible
- Every beat is a real camera at the real moment, in order; nothing is re-enacted or rendered.
- The team's reactions confirm each milestone.

## Recipe
Lay the mission audio first, cut picture in chronological order at 1.5-2.5 s per shot, alternate onboard and human views, no supers, end on the next event in two small caps lines.
