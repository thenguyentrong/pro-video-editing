## Core idea
European deep-tech restraint: monochrome or natural colour, monospaced "system" type, no adjectives. The reveal is cold and abstract; the proof films are field footage with the machine's own screen on view and short labels that read like test-report headings (capability, human in the loop, location).

## House rules (measured)
- **Two type voices:** monospaced caps for system/titles/locations (≈2.5-10 % H, typed on; closest free: JetBrains Mono / IBM Plex Mono / Space Mono) and a bold grotesk caps for capability labels (≈5 % H per line, 2 lines, bottom-left, no box; closest free: Inter 700). The reveal uses a light grotesk (≈5 % H, second word grey).
- **Brand mark on black** 2-6 s at the end (≈8-13 % H); in HX-2 a letterboxed proof shot follows after the logo.
- **Fast cutting:** ASL 1.05-1.54 s, median 0.6-1.28 s, 34-66 % of shots under 1 s. The longest shots are UI recordings or the final proof (4-6 s).
- **Colour:** reveal = monochrome teal-grey; proof = natural overcast daylight, untouched UI colours.
- **Sound:** no narrator in reveal/demo; interview voice in the explainer (≈2.2 words/s). Masters -11.9 to -15.4 LUFS, true peak above 0 dBTP. Drop-out 0.5-0.7 s before the title or logo.

## How they make trust visible
- The drone's real camera feed and status text (with clock time) are kept on screen 4-5 s at a time.
- Labels state the human role (operator in the loop) and separate manual from automatic modes.
- Partners named on screen with their logos; the test location named in the first seconds.
- Personnel faces blurred: operational reality instead of casting.
- An engineer at the site explains, not a voice-over artist.
- Weak spot: the reveal is all render-looking without a label; the capability montage is fast (0.9 s median), so each claim gets little screen time.

## Recipe
1. Reveal: light bars in black, 0.4-0.8 s fragments with 1-2 frame black dips, deceleration from 16 to 0 cuts per 10 s, name in light grotesk on black at 80 %, logo.
2. Field proof: 2 s real POV hook, 0.7 s silence, mono title typed; one 2-line caps label per claim held across 2-6 shots; real UI 4-5 s per mode; partner label + logo; blurred faces; logo; optional letterboxed proof after the logo.
3. Engineer film: aerial with a mono location super, 6 s of abstract signal graphics, mono title card, interview at the site over 70 % b-roll, 6 s logo.
