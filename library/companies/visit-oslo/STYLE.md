## Core idea
Two ways to film a city. The old way (This is Oslo): a bright drone montage with chapter captions. The award-winning way (the "even a city" films): one deadpan local in composed, still tableaux, complaining about things that are really benefits, with subtitles on every line and a quiet logo plate. The second way sells character and tone; the city proves him wrong in the background of every shot.

## Rules with numbers
- **Deadpan local**: ASL 2.7–3.3 s, median 2.1–3.0 s, 0–9 % of shots under 1 s; the character is still and centred; the city acts behind him.
- **Subtitles on every line**: white sans, cap ≈2.7 % H, translucent black box, bottom centre; they carry the jokes on mute.
- **One handwritten name super**: name ≈6 % H, role ≈3.8 % H, next to the subject, ≈2–2.5 s.
- **Logo plate on the last, long shot**: 8.5 s (first film) or the last 7.6 s of a 28.7 s oner (sequel); circled "OSLO" ≈12 % H, centred.
- **Montage version**: ASL ≈1.9 s, slowing every quarter (1.54 → 2.37); 8 captions of ≈2 s at 4.5 % H, bottom centre.
- **Sound**: voice films −16.5 / −13.7 LUFS; the musical sequel uses a 2.8 s silence before the chorus.

## Recipe (deadpan city film, 100 s)
1. Write 25–30 complaint lines that are secret benefits; end on a line that turns the tone ("a city should feel a little hard to get").
2. One shot per line, locked-off, the character centred or on a third, the city doing something behind him.
3. ASL ≈3.3 s; never under 1.5 s.
4. Subtitles on every line; one handwritten super at ≈20 % of the runtime.
5. Last shot 8 s at dusk with the logo fading in at +1 s.
6. Soft score under the voice; master −16 LUFS.
